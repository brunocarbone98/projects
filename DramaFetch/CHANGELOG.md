# Changelog

One version for the whole of DramaFetch — the Python tool (`pyproject.toml`, `dramafetch --version`) and the Chrome extension (`extension/manifest.json`, shown in `chrome://extensions` and next to the title of the DramaFetch tab and the popup). Every change users can see ships with a new number: a new feature raises the middle number (1.2.0 → 1.3.0), a fix the last one (1.2.0 → 1.2.1), and a release that removes features you had raises the first number (1.8.0 → 2.0.0). Dates are the day the version was finished.

## 4.0.2 — 2026-11-28

The owner's report of 2026-11-27, a screenshot of 4.0.1 downloading “I Found My Siren Boyfriend in My Bathtub” (7709866030612599814, 56 episodes), whose progress table read `episode 53 missing (gap)`, `TikTok listed 1 item DramaFetch could not read (no episode number)` and `TikTok announces 56 episodes, 55 listed`. A fix, hence the new last number.

- **What was wrong.** TikTok's list of that drama brought 56 items for its 56 episodes, one of them without a usable episode number, and episode 53 was the one number missing — so that item is, in all likelihood, episode 53 (the screenshot does not show the item itself, nor where it sat in the list). DramaFetch set aside every item without a number, taking it for a trailer or another clip that is no episode, so it named episode 53 missing, never downloaded it and never called the drama complete.
- **What 4.0.2 does.** An item TikTok lists without its number can now take the number of its place in TikTok's list — which is in episode order — when that place leaves no doubt, and only then:
  - first DramaFetch asks TikTok again for the page that should hold that episode (the same single request 4.0.1 made): if TikTok sends the episode with its number this time, nothing is numbered by its place — and only an answer that reaches that place counts;
  - the item must sit right after episode 52 or right before episode 54, with exactly its room between the two — a number listed on each side, the one missing number between them —, so an item before the first episode, after the last one, or between two consecutive episodes never takes a number;
  - it must carry a play address, be at least half as long as the shorter of the two episodes around it, and not be linked to another episode TikTok lists (a clip of it); no other item may claim the same number; and several such items side by side are numbered only together, when every one of them qualifies.
  It is then listed, counted against TikTok's count and downloaded like any other — `… - Episode 53.mp4`, the same checks, its cover in the MP4 —, and one line under the drama's header, muted, says so: `episode 53 came without its number: placed by its position in TikTok's list (between episodes 52 and 54)` (in Spanish, `el episodio 53 llegó sin su número: se ubicó por su posición en la lista de TikTok (entre los episodios 52 y 54)`). Several such items side by side fill a hole of exactly their number, in TikTok's order, wherever they fall in a page of TikTok's list. An item that does not meet all of this stays what it was — `TikTok listed 1 item DramaFetch could not read (no episode number)`, never downloaded — and its episode is still named missing, with its row and **Retry**. The extension's report of what did not download names each item numbered this way (`placed_items`).
- **What to do.** Paste the drama again — its link (any episode of it) or its ID: if TikTok's item of episode 53 sits in its place, episode 53 downloads, and every episode already on disk is skipped without a request (a drama with an episode missing was never marked complete, so nothing waits 24 hours). If the line still reads `TikTok listed 1 item DramaFetch could not read (no episode number)` and episode 53 is still missing, send the drama's report (`Downloads/DramaFetch/reports/`, or **Save the report**): its listing shows each page TikTok sent — what was asked, how many items came and which episode numbers —, so where the item without a number came from can be read off. With the Python tool, run the same `dramafetch download` again.
- **Permissions:** none new and none fewer, so Chrome shows no new warning. No new request: the one that asks TikTok again is the one 4.0.1 already made.
- **To update:** replace the files in the SAME folder and press the reload arrow on DramaFetch's card in `chrome://extensions` while the DramaFetch tab shows **idle**. Your settings, each drama's progress (what is on disk) and the cooldown stay.
- The Python tool is fixed the same way and carries the same version number: `info`, `download` and `serve` number such an item by its place (the browser strategy, which asks TikTok again; a page's embedded list never does — `auto` then lists the drama with the browser) and download it like any other.

## 4.0.1 — 2026-11-27

The owner's report of 2026-11-27, a screenshot of 4.0.0 on a drama of 66 episodes whose progress table read `episode 9 missing (gap)`, `episode 17 missing (gap)`, `TikTok listed 2 items DramaFetch could not read (a highlight, a clip of an episode)` and `TikTok announces 66 episodes, 64 listed` — “y los episodios existen” (and the episodes exist): both play on TikTok. A fix, hence the new last number.

- **What was wrong.** TikTok marks some items of a drama's episode list as highlights. 3.0.1 took every such item for a short clip of an episode, never for the episode itself: when no other item of the list had its number, DramaFetch set it aside (`TikTok listed 1 item DramaFetch could not read (a highlight, a clip of an episode)`, in Spanish `… (un fragmento destacado, un clip de un episodio)`), named its episode missing — `episode 9 missing (gap)`, a `missing from the list` row under **Did not download** — and asked TikTok again for it, which brought the same item back. But TikTok marks some full episodes this way: the owner's drama has 66 episodes, TikTok's list holds exactly 66 items, and the items of episodes 9 and 17 carry the mark — no other item has those numbers — and are those episodes. So 3.0.1 and 4.0.0 never downloaded such an episode (their **Retry** could not either), and never called the drama complete. Before 3.0.1, DramaFetch downloaded such an item as its episode: that was right.
- **What 4.0.1 does.** An item TikTok marks as a highlight that is the only item with its number is that number's episode: listed, counted against TikTok's count and downloaded like any other — same file name, same checks, its cover in the MP4 —, with no new message and no new row. When several such items share a number, DramaFetch keeps one by its usual order — one your account may play over a locked one, then the longer one, then the first listed — and says which it left out (`duplicate episode number 9: item … left out (shorter), item … kept`). An episode and a highlight with one number still keep the episode: the highlight is the item left out — `duplicate episode number 2: item … left out (a highlight), item … kept` —, as in 3.0.1. The message `… could not read (a highlight, a clip of an episode)` is gone. Everything else 3.0.1 and 4.0.0 do stays: the count against TikTok's own, asking TikTok again for what is missing, the `missing from the list` rows and their **Retry**, `TikTok's list may be incomplete`, the automatic retry, the alerts and the report.
- **What to do.** Paste the drama again — its link (any episode of it) or its ID: the episodes 4.0.0 said were missing download, and every episode already on disk is skipped without a request. A drama with an episode missing was never marked complete, so nothing waits 24 hours. With the Python tool, run the same `dramafetch download` again: it downloads those episodes and skips the ones on disk.
- **Permissions:** none new and none fewer, so Chrome shows no new warning.
- **To update:** replace the files in the SAME folder and press the reload arrow on DramaFetch's card in `chrome://extensions` while the DramaFetch tab shows **idle**. Your settings, each drama's progress (what is on disk) and the cooldown stay.
- The Python tool is fixed the same way and carries the same version number: `info`, `download` and `serve` list such an episode, and `download` and `serve` download it like any other.

## 4.0.0 — 2026-11-27

The owner's decision of 2026-11-27: “Quitemos el Library de DramaFetch” (take the Library out of DramaFetch) and, shown two sketches of the tab and two choices for your TikTok Favorites, “B y 1” — B: no library and no **See episodes**; 1: DramaFetch never reads your Favorites at all. Then: “permite que las descargas se hagan automáticamente al pegar el link o el id, y permitir las descargas masivas, es decir que se vayan acumulando en la cola” (let a download start by itself when a link or an ID is pasted, and allow mass downloads — they pile up in the queue): DramaFetch has done both since 1.7.0, and its box now says so. It removes things you had — your library, **See episodes** and everything DramaFetch read of your Favorites —, hence the new first number. What is left is the one way that always worked: paste, and the dramas download.

- **Gone: your library** (in Spanish, **Biblioteca**) — the cards of your TikTok **Favorites** → **Drama** with their covers, titles and lengths, the **Same title as another favorite** badge, **Queued** and **Downloading · 12/30** on a card, the search box, the sort and the sort DramaFetch kept, **Choose**, **Select all shown**, **Clear selection**, **Download selected dramas** with its `3 selected · 142 episodes`, a card's **Download**, **See episodes** and **Open on TikTok**, the connection line (`Read from your TikTok tab (@you) · 2 min ago · TikTok counts 946 dramas`) and **Update from TikTok** —, the **Library** link at the top of the tab (**Download** · **Settings** remain), and the popup's **Open my library** (**Abrir mi biblioteca**) with its hint on your profile. The popup shows **Open DramaFetch** and its hint everywhere, and the series with **Download this series** on a drama's page — nothing else.
- **Gone: DramaFetch reading your Favorites.** It no longer reads, watches or keeps anything of your TikTok Favorites — not the list, not TikTok's count, not the request your profile page makes for it — and no longer runs anything in a TikTok tab you opened yourself: the only TikTok tab it works in is the muted background tab it opens to list a drama, as before. Your Favorites stay exactly as they are on TikTok (DramaFetch has changed nothing in them since 3.0.0). To download a drama of your Favorites, open it on TikTok and use the toolbar button → **Download this series**, or copy its address into the box.
- **Gone: See episodes** (**Ver episodios**) — a drama's episode list with its covers, its `free` / `paid · unlocked` / `locked` and `on disk` badges, **Download all available** and **Download selected** —, and with it 3.0.0's download again in full. A drama always downloads whole: you can no longer pick single episodes, and nothing is downloaded again over a file you have — every download skips, without a request, the episodes already on disk. To get an episode again (one that plays badly, say), delete its file, then paste the drama's link or ID: the missing episode is downloaded, the others are skipped. To ask TikTok again for locked episodes — or for one DramaFetch found protected by DRM, as its message says —, tick **Also ask for locked episodes** under the box and paste the drama. The **Waiting** rows `Download selected E01, E02, E05 — …`, `Download all available — …`, `See episodes — …` and `Update from TikTok` go with them. The tab no longer shows any episode cover; each episode's cover still goes into its MP4, as your **Cover** setting says.
- **The box says what it does** (the owner's request of 2026-11-27; the behaviour is 1.7.0's). Paste a drama's link (any episode of it) or its ID and the whole drama downloads at once, no button to press; paste several, one per line, and they download one after the other; paste more while DramaFetch works and they join the queue — **Waiting** lists every drama still to run, in order, by its name once DramaFetch knows it and its whole ID, and each downloads in its turn, with every pause. A drama already downloading or waiting is never queued twice (the line under the box names it). Typed IDs: press Enter. During the 30-minute cooldown, or after Chrome refused to save DramaFetch's progress, a paste starts nothing: it stays in the box, one per line, for Enter later. Its label and hint now say so, in English and Spanish: `Paste one or more series links (any episode of each) or series IDs, one per line: each whole drama downloads at once, one after the other` and `Keep pasting while DramaFetch works: what you paste joins the queue (Waiting) and downloads in its turn, with the usual pauses …` (in Spanish, `Pega uno o varios enlaces de serie (de cualquier episodio) o IDs de serie, uno por línea: …` and `Sigue pegando mientras DramaFetch trabaja: lo que pegues se suma a la cola (En espera) …`).
- **Every fix of 3.0.1 is in 4.0.0** (below; the owner's report of 2026-11-27: free episodes missing without a word). DramaFetch counts each drama's episodes against TikTok's own count, asks TikTok again — at most 4 times, at the list's own pace — for the list pages that should hold what is missing, and says what is still missing: a `missing from the list` row under **Did not download** with its **Retry**, or `TikTok's list may be incomplete` when it cannot tell; a free episode it only guessed was locked reads `not downloaded (free)`, with a **Retry** that asks TikTok for it; the tab keeps every paste's line and its **Did not download** rows until you reload it, and the batch table has a **Not asked** column. If you update from 3.0.0 or earlier, the first paste of a drama found complete in the last 24 hours lists it once more, as 3.0.1 says. Where 3.0.1's entry speaks of **See episodes**, 4.0.0 removed it: the list's warnings show only under the drama's row in the progress table, and **Stop** keeps the block true as 3.0.1 says for a **Retry**, an automatic retry and a batch — a paste, Enter or **Download this series**; where it says the library stays when you update, 4.0.0 deletes it (**To update**, below).
- **Unchanged from 3.0.1:** the toolbar's **Download this series**, the progress card — the list's warnings under each drama, and every job's line and its rows under **Did not download (N)** kept until you reload the tab —, **Did not download (N)** and its **Retry**, the automatic retry (1.8.0), the alerts and their **Retry** — which name apart the episodes TikTok's list lacked and the free ones DramaFetch did not ask for —, the 30-minute cooldown and its banner, **Stop**, the settings, the report of what did not download, and the 24-hour skip of a drama found complete with **Look again at series found complete in the last 24 hours**. Every **Retry** — the tab's, an alert's, the automatic one — is again what it was before 3.0.0: an episode meanwhile on disk is skipped (and, as in 3.0.1, the tab's and an alert's **Retry** ask TikTok anyway for a free episode DramaFetch only guessed was locked — the automatic one never does).
- **Permissions:** none new and none fewer — `scripting`, `tabs` and `webRequest` keep their use for the background tab DramaFetch opens to list a drama, `notifications` for the alerts —, so Chrome shows no new warning.
- **To update:** replace the files in the SAME folder and press the reload arrow on DramaFetch's card in `chrome://extensions` while the DramaFetch tab shows **idle**. The first time the DramaFetch tab starts after the update, it deletes from Chrome's storage the library 3.x kept there (its dramas' IDs, titles and episode counts, and your profile's `@handle`) and the library's sort; nothing else is deleted — your settings, each drama's progress (what is on disk) and the cooldown stay. A DramaFetch tab Chrome restores at an address of 3.x (the library's, or your Favorites') opens as usual, with nothing queued. A cooldown that a 3.x reading of your Favorites started keeps its English reason in a Spanish Chrome until it ends (at most 30 minutes after the update).
- The Python tool changes nothing beyond 3.0.1's fixes and carries the same version number: `dramafetch serve` still lists a drama's episodes with their covers and downloads all of them or a selection, `download --episodes` still picks episodes, and `--overwrite` still downloads again the files you have.

## 3.0.1 — 2026-11-27

The owner's report of 2026-11-27: “dramafetch no descarga todos los episodios de ciertos dramas. No manda mensaje de error … simplemente los hace skip a uno o dos episodios … y son episodios gratis” — some dramas lost one or two free episodes (“a veces es un episodio 28 de una lista de 66, [a veces] el último de la lista”) with no error and no row in the tab — and “que dramafetch cuente y se dé cuenta si falta un episodio, si de 5 salta al 7 sin contar el 6” (count, and notice when an episode is missing). A fix, hence the new last number. DramaFetch's way of reading a drama's episode list had not changed since 2.0.1, so the likeliest trigger is a change in what TikTok serves; 3.0.1 does not depend on knowing which: it counts, asks again, and says what is still missing — and, when it cannot tell whether an episode is missing, it says that too.

- **What was wrong.** An episode could go missing in three ways, none of them said:
  - When TikTok's episode list came short — without one of its pages (the drama's last, or one in the middle), or a page without one of its episodes —, DramaFetch took the list as it came, so those episodes were never asked for: no row, no message, and the drama could even read complete.
  - A free episode whose list entry only LOOKED locked to DramaFetch's own rules — its sound in the list shorter than the video (`plays 69 s of 106 s`), or no play address — was skipped before any request as `locked, skipped`, and that counted as a fine outcome: the drama read ✓ complete, with no row under **Did not download**, no **Retry** and no report.
  - Every paste is a job of its own, and the next one cleared the tab: drama A's line, its rows under **Did not download** and its **Retry** were gone as soon as drama B started (only its alert stayed).
  - And smaller ones: an item whose episode number TikTok sent empty or as 0 was dropped; of two items with one number (a highlight clip and its episode) whichever came first was kept, and a highlight clip listed without its episode was saved as that episode; a link to a later episode opened the drama at that episode.
- **DramaFetch counts now.** TikTok says how many episodes a drama has (`numVideos`, on every episode of its list). Once the list is read, DramaFetch looks for what is missing: a hole (5, then 7 without 6), the last episodes up to TikTok's count, or a list that stopped before TikTok said it was over. When something is missing it asks TikTok again, before any download, for the list pages that should hold it — the very request the list itself makes, after the same 0.8–1.6 s pause, one at a time and at most 4 per listing; nothing faster, no new kind of request, and a 429 or a verification stops it as always, with the 30-minute cooldown. When TikTok's new answer holds the episode, the drama downloads whole and nothing more is said.
  - **Only an episode that is certainly missing is named missing.** A list can carry other items besides the episodes — a trailer, a highlight clip —, and whether TikTok's count includes them is not known yet. So DramaFetch names as missing only the episodes up to TikTok's count less those other items — or up to the number of a highlight clip listed without its episode, within TikTok's count (that episode exists) —: never a row for an episode that may not exist. A drama TikTok counts 66 whose list brings 65 episodes and a trailer may have them all (TikTok counting the trailer) or lack its last one; DramaFetch then says it cannot tell (`TikTok's list may be incomplete`, below) instead of guessing either way — and never calls such a drama complete.
  - A link to any episode of a drama lists it from episode 1, so the whole drama downloads. The refresh of expired play addresses in the middle of a download (the extension, and the Python tool's `--engine browser`) lists the drama the same way: from episode 1, the whole list at the list's own pace, asking again included, before it takes the episode it needs.
  - An episode number TikTok sends empty or as 0 is read from the episode's other number. Of two items with one number DramaFetch keeps the real episode over a highlight clip, then one your account may play over a locked one, then the longer one, else the first listed — and says which item it left out. A highlight clip is never saved as an episode: one listed without its episode is set aside — `TikTok listed 1 item DramaFetch could not read (a highlight, a clip of an episode)` (`… (un fragmento destacado, un clip de un episodio)`) — and its episode counts as not listed, like any episode the list lacks. Items it cannot read (a trailer, another drama's episode) are named in the list's warnings — `TikTok listed 1 item DramaFetch could not read (no episode number)` —, shown muted while everything TikTok counts is placed: when TikTok's count may include them, the list reads `may be incomplete` (below) and its warnings take the warning colour. An item TikTok sends without an id counts once when a later page brings it again unchanged, and a list page that comes back empty where TikTok had said there was more no longer passes for the end of the list.
- **What you see when TikTok still leaves an episode out.**
  - The drama's line under the table names it: `incomplete: 65 of 66 — episode 28 missing from TikTok's list`, or, when the list stopped early, `incomplete: 60 of 62 — TikTok's list ended early: episodes 61, 62 not received` (in a Spanish Chrome, `falta el episodio 28 en la lista de TikTok`, `la lista de TikTok terminó antes de tiempo: no llegaron los episodios 61, 62`).
  - Each missing episode gets a row under **Did not download** — the badge `missing from the list` (`falta en la lista`), the message `not in TikTok's list` —, so it gets the block's **Retry** (a fresh listing of the drama, asking again included, then those episodes), the alert's **Retry**, the report and the automatic retry (1.8.0), once, under its usual rules (never after a stop, never during the cooldown). A retry whose fresh list still lacks an episode gives it a row of its own, `not in TikTok's list this time`, instead of nothing.
  - The list's warnings show under the drama's row in the progress table for a paste too, not only in **See episodes**: muted when they are only information, in the warning colour when the list is short or may be. `TikTok announces 62 episodes, 60 listed` shows whenever TikTok's count and the episodes listed differ.
  - Its alert is ⚠ — `⚠ <drama> — 2 episodes to retry` / `E61, E62 are not in TikTok's list.`, with **Retry** —, or `⚠ <drama> — TikTok's list may be incomplete` when the list is short with nothing to retry (say, it stopped early and TikTok gave no count, or DramaFetch cannot tell: next point).
  - The report of what did not download is saved for such a drama too, with a new `listing` section: every list page asked for (its cursor and count, how many items it brought and their episode numbers, whether TikTok said there was more, the cursor it returned), the requests that asked again, the items DramaFetch could not read and why, the list's warnings, TikTok's count and the other items it may be counting — the list's address without its query, no cookie, nothing signed. Its `Report saved: <file>` line and **Save the report** show under the progress table even when no episode got a row (then without the block's heading, table and **Retry**). Attach it when asking for help.
  - A drama with an episode missing is never marked complete, so its next paste lists it again instead of skipping it for 24 hours.
- **When DramaFetch cannot tell: `TikTok's list may be incomplete`.** When TikTok counts more episodes than DramaFetch can place — more than the highest one listed, and the list also carries a trailer or a highlight that TikTok may be counting —, DramaFetch asks TikTok once more for the page after the last episode listed (one of the 4 requests). When that brings nothing new:
  - the list's warning says so, in the warning colour: `TikTok's list may be incomplete: TikTok counts 66 episodes; 65 episodes and 1 other item (a trailer or a highlight) were listed, so DramaFetch cannot tell whether an episode is missing — paste the drama again later to check again` (in a Spanish Chrome, `puede que la lista de TikTok esté incompleta: …`), and the drama's line ends `TikTok's list may be incomplete: TikTok counts 66 episodes, 65 listed and 1 other item (a trailer or a highlight)`;
  - its alert is `⚠ <drama> — TikTok's list may be incomplete`, with the line as its message and no **Retry** — no episode gets a row under **Did not download**: none is certainly missing —, and the report is saved, its `listing` section holding TikTok's count, the episodes listed and the other items;
  - the drama is never marked complete, so its next paste — or run — lists it again: one ordinary listing with its pauses; what is on disk is skipped without a request;
  - what to do: paste the drama again later (with the Python tool, run the same `download` again): if TikTok's next list brings the episode, it downloads. To know whether one is missing, compare with the episode count tiktok.com or the TikTok app shows for the drama. When asking for help, attach the saved report and say how many episodes the TikTok app shows for the drama: the two together settle whether TikTok counts the trailer. Until that is settled, a drama whose count does include its trailer or highlight reads `may be incomplete` every time it is listed. A drama with an episode certainly missing too gets both: that episode's row, and the warning.
- **A free episode DramaFetch only guessed was locked is reported as not downloaded.** The first download still does not ask for it (no request, as before), but it no longer passes for done: its row reads `not downloaded (free)` (`no descargado (gratis)`), in the failure colour, under **Did not download**, with what the list said of it as its message; the drama's line says `1 free episode not asked for (3): TikTok's list made it look locked`; its alert is ⚠ — `E03 was not asked for: TikTok's list made it look locked (plays 69 s of 106 s).` — with **Retry**; the report is saved, and the drama is not marked complete. Its **Retry** asks TikTok for it anyway — one request, the usual pace; TikTok decides: a whole file is saved, a short one ends `TikTok serves this episode short: …` and nothing is saved — as long as a fresh list still shows nothing but DramaFetch's guess: one TikTok now marks with a price, a lock or DRM stays skipped. The automatic retry never asks for such an episode on its own, but its alerts name it: when it retries the drama's other episodes, `↻ <drama> — retrying 1 episode` says `E05 did not download — DramaFetch tries it once more on its own. E03 was not asked for: TikTok's list made it look locked (plays 69 s of 106 s).`, and when that retry never runs, is refused, is stopped or gets no fresh list (a login wall, no episode list in time), the ⚠ alert that replaces it names the free episode beside what is left, its **Retry** asking for it too — never a `not complete` alert without a **Retry**. Paid episodes your account has not unlocked, episodes TikTok itself marks locked and DRM keep `locked, skipped` exactly as before, and **Also ask for locked episodes** is unchanged.
  - The batch table under the progress card counts such episodes in a column of their own, **Not asked** (**Sin pedir**), between **Locked** and **Failed** — **Locked** keeps the paid, TikTok-flagged and DRM ones. On a narrow window the count columns' headings turn sideways, so the table still fits (from 320 px wide).
- **The first paste of a drama after the update lists it again.** A drama found complete by 3.0.0 or earlier may have lost its last episodes, or a free one, under the old rules, and its 24-hour skip would hide that. A complete mark now records the rule it was written under; an older one is still read but never skips: that drama is listed once more — one ordinary listing with its pauses; what is on disk is skipped without a request — and, found whole, marked anew, so the paste after that is skipped for 24 hours as before. A **Retry** that saves a drama's last missing episode does not mark it complete itself: its next paste lists it once more and marks it.
- **The tab keeps every job's outcome.** The lines under the progress table stay for the tab's life (the newest 500), each coloured by its outcome — complete, or incomplete. The **Did not download** block keeps every drama's rows across jobs: a job's end replaces only the rows of the episodes it took — a drama's retry removes what it saved and keeps what is still not downloaded, with its new message — and leaves every other drama's; its heading counts every row and its **Retry** retries them all (one job per drama, and one more for its free episodes not asked for). The progress table still shows the job that runs, **Save the report** still saves the last job's report, and reloading the tab clears everything, as before.
  - **Stop** keeps the block true too: the dramas a stopped batch had finished get their rows; a **Retry**, an automatic retry or a **See episodes** download you stop takes out of the block what it saved, an episode that failed before the **Stop** keeps or gets its row with its new message, and the episodes it never reached keep theirs; the drama a batch was downloading when you pressed **Stop** loses the rows of the episodes it saved and gets no new ones — download it again. A job you end with **Stop** saves no report, as before.
- **The Python tool** is fixed the same way: the same listing from episode 1, the same asking again (the browser strategy; `auto` hands it a list the page embeds only in part, or one that may be incomplete), the same reading of the list's items and the same report line — `download` prints the list's warnings, and its line names the missing episodes, the free ones not asked for (`--include-locked` asks for those) and a list that may be incomplete. `download --episodes` gives a requested episode TikTok's list lacks — up to the drama's last episode as far as anyone knows — a row, `failed-unavailable` with `not in TikTok's list this time` (exit 2), instead of only a note; the table after several series has a `not asked` column between `locked` and `failed`; `serve`'s page colours a free episode not asked for as not downloaded, and its job reports carry `uncertain` and `other_items`; and a complete mark in `dramafetch.json` written before 3.0.1 never skips: the next run lists that series again. `--strategy auto` (the default) stops at a 429 or a verification TikTok raises while the browser completes the list — exit 5 or 4, with the 30-minute cooldown, as anywhere else — instead of going on with the part the page embedded. Either a window for an episode this account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side — the list cannot tell: …`) and gains an `episodes_digest` per series: for every listed episode its number, access, hints, duration, `music.duration`, size, bitrate and the `IsPreview` / `PreviewType` marks — no URLs — so a wrong decision can be checked from the report alone. Its cause for a shorter file of a size the list did not announce states what was measured and both readings too (`the file plays 70 s where the list announces 106 s, and its size is none the list announced — a shorter file than listed: a window or a preview clip for an episode this account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side; …`), instead of calling it a preview for an episode the account has not unlocked.
