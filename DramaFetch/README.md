# DramaFetch

[![CI](https://github.com/brunocarbone98/projects/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/brunocarbone98/projects/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

DramaFetch downloads the episodes of a TikTok short drama that your own TikTok account can already watch — a whole series at a time instead of one video after another — for personal offline viewing. You paste the series URL; it discovers the episode list the way TikTok's own web app does, hands each episode to [yt-dlp](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp) — or, with `--engine browser`, fetches it through one browser session of its own — paces itself like one person watching, remembers what it already has so interrupted runs resume, downloads the paid episodes your own account has unlocked like free ones, and lists (then skips) the episodes it has not unlocked. It has three front ends: a CLI and a small local web UI, which drive the same engine, and — without Python — [DramaFetch for Chrome](#chrome-extension-windows-macos-linux), a Chrome extension that re-implements that engine with the same rules and does the same from the Chrome you are logged in to.

> **Legal notice.** DramaFetch is for personal, offline viewing of episodes that your own TikTok account can already watch. It does not unlock paid episodes and does not bypass DRM or access controls; locked episodes are listed and skipped. Downloading may breach TikTok's Terms of Service, and the videos belong to their creators and studios — you are responsible for how you use this tool.

> **Built without access to TikTok.** This tool was written in a sandbox that cannot reach tiktok.com. Every network-facing piece is thin and injectable, tested against hand-written payload fixtures that follow the documented `item_list` shape and a local fake TikTok server — never against TikTok itself. The first real run is the real test: follow [Before your first real run](#before-your-first-real-run) — `dramafetch doctor`, then `dramafetch info URL --cookies cookies.txt --strategy browser --dump-raw raw/` on one short series, then three episodes — and read [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting) if the episode list looks wrong.

## How it works

Verified facts (October 2026) that the code depends on. They are also recorded in the docstring of `src/dramafetch/urls.py`; when TikTok changes, update both (see [`CLAUDE.md`](./CLAUDE.md)).

- A series URL looks like `https://www.tiktok.com/shortdrama/episode/7675706520839033876/8`. The long number is the **series id** (TikTok calls it `dramaID` in its API), _not_ a video id; the trailing number is only the 1-based episode you were watching.
- yt-dlp does not recognise `/shortdrama/` URLs ([yt-dlp/yt-dlp#17725](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/17725), open) — that gap is why this tool exists. yt-dlp _does_ download each episode's ordinary video page, `https://www.tiktok.com/@<user>/video/<item_id>`; the `@user` segment is not checked, so `@_` works.
- TikTok's web app lists episodes through `GET /api/drama/episode/item_list/?dramaID=<series_id>&cursor=<c>&count=<n>&…` (plus its usual web parameters and signatures). Each item carries the episode's video id, `dramaInfo.dramaID` and `dramaInfo.DramaVideoData.EpisodeNumber`; episode order comes from `EpisodeNumber`, never from upload time, and items whose `dramaID` differs from the series are dropped. `GET /api/drama/detail/` adds the series name and the announced total, which is how gaps are detected.
- DramaFetch therefore opens the series page in a real Chromium (Playwright), captures those API responses as the page makes them, paginates the way the page does — same request, next `cursor` — and builds the episode list. That Chromium is Playwright's full browser, launched headless with its default flags and your machine's locale — not Playwright's `chromium-headless-shell`, which it starts by default for a headless run since Playwright 1.49 and which announces `HeadlessChrome` in `Sec-CH-UA` and `navigator.userAgentData` whatever user agent is set; `--browser-channel chrome` or `msedge` uses the Chrome or Edge you have installed instead. A captcha met while the list is paginated — after the first page too — stops the run (exit 4, the 30-minute cooldown) instead of downloading from a partial list. A static strategy reads the JSON TikTok embeds in the page (`__UNIVERSAL_DATA_FOR_REHYDRATION__`) when that is enough — fetched through curl_cffi's impersonated Chrome when it is installed, which sends its own Chrome headers, consistent with its TLS fingerprint (DramaFetch adds the cookies only), else through urllib under the Chrome user agent; `--strategy auto` tries static first and falls back to the browser.
- Without a logged-in TikTok session the list endpoints return nothing and the videos have no play address; yt-dlp then reports "No video formats found" or lands on `/login`. A `cookies.txt` exported from your logged-in browser is the normal way to run (see [Cookies](#cookies-you-will-need-them)). Cookie values are never printed, logged, dumped or sent anywhere but TikTok.
- Paid episodes: TikTok sells later episodes (coins, a purchase, a subscription), and the episode list it sends to YOUR session says what that session may play. A paid episode your account has unlocked — an entitlement flag such as `isUnlocked: true`, or a play URL TikTok gave your session for a sold episode — is `unlocked` and downloads like a free one, with your cookies; one it has not unlocked — a DRM flag always, and, when no entitlement flag says your account may play it, a short header (next bullet), a lock or paywall flag, an entitlement flag that says no (`isUnlocked: false`) or no play address at all — is `locked`, listed with its hints and skipped by default. Nothing in DramaFetch unlocks, buys, bypasses or fakes entitlement, and nothing decrypts DRM — see [Paid episodes](#paid-episodes).
- Free series with conditions, and the short header (verified 2026-11-06 on the owner's real run: 62 episodes of a series TikTok marks `dramaInfo.isLimitedFree: true` — free, with conditions the field does not name). Three fields of such a list say nothing about what your session may play and give no clue: `DramaVideoData.IsFreeIntro` ("is this the free intro teaser" — false on every episode, the first included; not "is free"), `IsPreview` and `PreviewType` (true, `4` or `1` on fully served episodes too). What does say something is the durations: `video.duration` is the episode's full length, and `music.duration` (the episode's own sound, `music.original: true`) is how long that sound lasts — equal to `video.duration` on a fully served episode, about 70 s on one TikTok serves short: a file of exactly the listed size (`video.size`, the whole episode by bytes) whose MP4 header plays only that part. That can be a window for an episode the account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side — the owner's 7 episodes of 2026-11-06 broke at the same point in the TikTok app and in TikTok's web player: no sound past it, a broken picture when seeking past it — and the list cannot tell the two apart. DramaFetch reads the tell before any request — such an episode is `locked` with the hint `plays 69 s of 106 s` and skipped — and names the file after a download when the list did not give it away (`TikTok serves this episode short: the whole file came (9676471 bytes, as listed) but it plays 70 s of 106 s — if the TikTok app plays it whole, watch it there, then --recheck looks again; if the app breaks at the same point, the episode is defective on TikTok's side and no retry changes it`, status `failed-unavailable`, the file kept as `<name> (short).mp4`). DramaFetch never rewrites a file's header to play past what it indexes — where that is TikTok's access control, it is the line. If the TikTok app plays the episode whole, watch it there with the same account, then look again (`--recheck`, **Refresh list**, the extension's **Look again** checkbox) and the next list reads it fresh; if the app breaks at the same point too, nothing changes it until TikTok replaces the file.
- Politeness: one download at a time, a random 4–9 s pause between episodes, a 60–120 s rest every 10 downloads, 0.8–1.6 s between list pages, a full stop on HTTP 403, HTTP 429 or three failures in a row — and, after a 403, a 429 or a captcha, a 30-minute cooldown during which nothing contacts TikTok at all — see [Pacing and TikTok's protections](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections).
- Integrity: every saved video is checked (a parseable MP4, not cut short, with audio, about as long as the list says) before its cover is handled; a file that fails is kept as `<name> (incomplete).mp4`, the episode is `failed` and a rerun tries it again; an encrypted (DRM) video is never kept, whatever the flags — see [Integrity](#file-names-and-thumbnails).

## Pacing and TikTok's protections

TikTok protects its site against bulk downloading, and a whole series is 30+ requests in a row — exactly what that protection looks for. DramaFetch does not try to defeat it (no proxies, no stealth, no parallelism); it behaves like one person watching a series, which is the only approach that keeps working:

- **One request at a time.** Episodes are downloaded strictly one after another, in ascending order — in the CLI and in the web UI, where jobs queue. Never run two instances at once.
- **One series at a time, each resolved right before it is downloaded.** `download URL URL …` (or `--from-file`) runs its series strictly one after another and fetches a series' episode list only when its turn comes — never all the lists up front, which would be a burst of page loads and would let the signed play URLs expire before their episodes are reached. The web UI's **Several series** box does the same: one job per series, each resolving its own list when its turn comes ([Web UI](#web-ui)).
- **A pause between episodes.** A random 4–9 s by default (`--delay-min` / `--delay-max`). `--pace human` waits instead about as long as the episode you just downloaded lasts — its duration, at least `--delay-min`, plus 0–3 s of jitter, at most 300 s — the rhythm of someone actually watching.
- **A longer rest every few episodes.** After every 10 episodes that needed the network — a failed attempt counts too — (`--rest-every`, 0 disables) a 60–120 s rest (`--rest-min` / `--rest-max`).
- **A rest between series.** Between two series of a batch that both contact TikTok, the same 60–120 s rest (`--rest-min` / `--rest-max`) comes before the next list is fetched (`… resting 95 s before the next series`). The first series of a batch needs none, and neither does a series that makes no request (complete on disk, see below) — the next one that does still rests after the last one that did. The web UI rests the same way between two jobs that both contact TikTok, unless the earlier one finished more than `--rest-max` seconds before; a job of the **Several series** box counts as contacting TikTok from its list on, even when that list then comes from the 15-minute cache — the rest errs on the slow side.
- **A long break every 100 episodes.** Across a whole run — every series of a batch, or the web UI's whole lifetime — every 100 episodes that needed the network bring a 15–30 minute break (`… long break 22 min after 100 episodes`) in place of that step's pause: nobody watches 100 episodes in one sitting. It is not adjustable — no flag lowers it — and 15 minutes without any request count as the break, so the count then starts again. When it falls between two series — two series of a batch, or two batch jobs of the web UI — it replaces the rest between them and comes before the next list, so fresh play URLs never wait through it.
- **Randomised pauses between list pages.** While the episode list is discovered, the pause before each further `item_list` page is 0.8–1.6 s: the 0.8 s minimum times a random factor between 1 and 2, drawn afresh every time (the hidden `--page-delay` sets the minimum), so paging never ticks like a metronome.
- **Stop at the first sign of trouble.** An HTTP 403 (status `failed-blocked`), a JS challenge TikTok serves instead of a video page (`failed-blocked` too: yt-dlp's TikTok extractor would answer it — DramaFetch stops it before it computes or sends anything), an HTTP 429 (`failed-rate-limited`) or a login redirect (`failed-login-required`) stops the run at once, and so do 3 failures in a row (`--max-consecutive-failures`, 0 disables) — in a row across the series of a run, so a batch whose series each have one or two episodes left still stops at the third (a `done` resets the count, and so do 15 minutes without a request). The remaining episodes are marked `stopped: …` without one more request; nothing retries on its own. Wait 30–60 minutes, then run the same command again: the manifest resumes where the run stopped.
- **A batch stops at the first sign of trouble.** Whatever stops a series — a 403, a 429, a login redirect, 3 failures in a row, a captcha or any other failure to fetch its list, a cooldown — ends the whole batch: the remaining series are reported `not started: batch stopped in <series> (<reason>)` and get no request at all, not even their list. The cooldown is asked again before every series and before every episode, so a refusal another DramaFetch met meanwhile — during a pause or the long break — stops the run before its next request (`stopped: cooldown until 14:32`). A `cookies.txt` with malformed lines (see [Cookies](#cookies-you-will-need-them)) stops the batch too, before the first list is loaded: every download would refuse it. In the web UI the same stop cancels every job still waiting — the rest of the batch and any other queued job — with the same `not started: …` message.
- **Never knock again right after a refusal.** A refusal — HTTP 403 or 429 from a download, a JS challenge on a video page, HTTP 429 or a captcha page while the list is being resolved, before its first page or after it — also starts a **30-minute cooldown**: until it ends, `info`, `download` and `video` exit 5 before making a single request (`error: TikTok refused a request at 14:02 (…); DramaFetch waits until 14:32 (27 min left) before contacting TikTok again — delete … to lift it, at your own risk`), a run already under way — a batch in another terminal, a job of the web UI — stops before its next request, the web UI answers 429 and `dramafetch doctor` shows a `cooldown` line. The cooldown is one small file, `~/.dramafetch/cooldown.json` (`DRAMAFETCH_STATE_DIR` relocates the folder), shared by every DramaFetch command on the machine — the CLI and the web UI. The [Chrome extension](#chrome-extension-windows-macos-linux) keeps a cooldown of its own in Chrome's storage, and neither sees the other's: after a refusal in one of them, do not use the other on the same account until the time it shows. There is deliberately no flag to skip it: knocking again right after a refusal is exactly what makes TikTok's protection escalate from one refused request to a blocked session or address, and a flag would be used in that very moment. Deleting the file lifts it — at your own risk.
- **Fewer requests overall.** Skips (manifest, locked, a DRM verdict an earlier run found, existing file) cost nothing; yt-dlp is held to at most 3 retries with a one-second gap between its own requests; and `--engine browser` (next paragraph) downloads a series with about half the requests.
- **A complete series costs nothing for 24 hours.** When a whole-series run (`download` without `--episodes`, or a job of the web UI's **Several series** box) finds a series complete — every free or unlocked episode on disk, nothing failed, no gap in TikTok's list, as many listed as TikTok announces — its manifest remembers when (`complete_at`). For the next 24 hours that series is skipped without a single request, as long as every file it counted — at least one — is still on disk (`complete, nothing to do: 30 episodes already on disk (checked 2 h ago)`), so running the same batch again costs nothing for the series already done. A deleted file, `--recheck` (on `download` and `serve`), `--episodes`, `--overwrite`, `--include-locked` or `--dry-run` resolves the list as usual, and any new attempt (a new episode, a failure) makes the manifest forget the mark. When some of its episodes were locked, the line says so and points at `--recheck` — after unlocking episodes in the TikTok app, that is how they get downloaded (see [Paid episodes](#paid-episodes)).
- **One list per quarter of an hour in the web UI.** `serve` keeps every resolved episode list in memory for 15 minutes: **Resolve** followed by **Download all available** costs one discovery, not two, and resolving the same series again within that time costs nothing (**Refresh list** fetches it again); a batch job whose list is in that cache takes it from there too. A list is kept for the session that resolved it only: export `cookies.txt` again — another account, or yours after unlocking an episode in the TikTok app — and the next resolve, download or batch job fetches the list anew — see [Web UI](#web-ui).

**Fewer requests: `--engine browser`.** The episode list TikTok's web app loads already names each episode's signed play URLs (`video.bitrateInfo[*].PlayAddr.UrlList` and `video.playAddr`, valid for a few hours — the `expire` parameter). The default engine ignores them and hands every episode to yt-dlp, which loads the episode's video page and then the video, from a second client. `--engine browser` instead fetches those play URLs directly, through one Chromium session of its own, set up like the discovery's — your `cookies.txt`, the same Chrome user agent, the full Chromium launched headless —, one `GET` per episode (plus one small one for the cover, unless `--thumbnail none`): no per-episode page load, about half the requests. Those media and cover `GET`s are issued by Playwright's request client — Node's HTTP stack, carrying the context's cookies and Chrome user agent — not by Chromium's own network stack: fewer requests and one session's cookies, but not a browser's TLS/HTTP2 fingerprint for the media bytes. When the URLs have expired (a long run, a list resolved a while ago) it opens the series page once more in that same session, takes the fresh list and keeps it for the rest of the run, so a 30-episode run refreshes once, not 30 times. The fresh list is read like the first one: an episode it now calls locked — a lapsed subscription, a short header, a lock or paywall flag, DRM — is not fetched from it (none of its URLs is requested; `failed-unavailable`, `… (locked in a fresh episode list: <hints>)`) unless `--include-locked`. A 403, 404 or 410 on a URL moves on to the episode's next listed URL, then to a fresh list, and only then is the episode `failed-unavailable` — within a budget of 4 requests per episode, so a locked or region-restricted episode never becomes a burst of requests against every mirror; any other HTTP error fails the episode at once (no next URL, no fresh list), and so does a transfer cut short — fewer bytes than the announced `Content-Length`, or a connection the server dropped — (`failed`, `download looks incomplete or wrong: truncated body …`: the network failed, not the URL, so no other mirror is knocked). A 429 is `failed-rate-limited` and stops the run as usual, and so does a captcha page met while refreshing the list (`failed-blocked`, exit 5: once the cooldown has ended, rerun with `--headful` and solve it). Use it when yt-dlp trips the protection (HTTP 403, `failed-blocked`): `dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --engine browser`. `--browser-channel`, `--browser-path` and `--headful` apply to this engine too; each episode is fetched into memory (short-drama episodes are a few tens of MB), written as `.part` and renamed when complete; the engine needs the episode list, so it is for `download` and `serve`, not for `video`. It downloads only what the list offers to your account — nothing is unlocked, re-signed or guessed.

Pauses are printed on stderr as they happen (`… pausing 7 s`, `… resting 95 s after 10 downloads`, `… resting 95 s before the next series`, `… long break 22 min after 100 episodes`), and so is the reason a run stopped, so a quiet terminal is not a stuck one; `dramafetch doctor` prints the defaults in one line and whether a cooldown is in force (`– cooldown: none` / `✗ cooldown: until 14:32 (27 min left): <reason>`). The thresholds are TikTok's and unknown, and they change: the defaults are a floor, not a recommendation. For a long series raise them — `--delay-min 8 --delay-max 20 --rest-every 5`, or `--pace human` — and let the run take an hour; that is still faster than a 403 followed by a 30–60 minute wait. Mind the volume too: 10 series of 80 episodes in one day is about 2,400 requests over about 8 hours — far faster than anyone can watch, and visible as such. Spread a big batch over days, one or two series per sitting, with `--pace human`.

## Chrome extension (Windows, macOS, Linux)

**DramaFetch for Chrome** is the same tool as a Chrome extension: no Python, no command prompt and no `cookies.txt` — it works with the TikTok session of the Chrome you are logged in to. It lives in [`extension/`](./extension) and re-implements the engine in plain JavaScript with the Python tool's rules, texts and constants (a parity test compares them): whole series, several per run; `<Series name> - Episode NN.mp4` in a folder per series; the file exactly as TikTok serves it (nothing is re-encoded, picture and sound stay in sync) with its cover embedded as the MP4's cover art; every file checked before it is saved; paid episodes your own account has unlocked downloaded like free ones, locked ones listed and skipped, DRM detected and never kept; the same pacing, stops and 30-minute cooldown; interrupted runs resume. The [legal notice](#dramafetch) applies unchanged and is in the extension's footer.

**Install (once).**

1. Download this repository as a ZIP (on GitHub: **Code → Download ZIP**) — or the `dramafetch-extension` artifact of the latest green CI run (**Actions → CI →** the run **→ Artifacts**; GitHub asks you to sign in) — and unzip it.
2. Open `chrome://extensions`, turn on **Developer mode** (top right), click **Load unpacked** and select the folder that DIRECTLY contains `manifest.json` — a folder above it makes Chrome answer that the manifest file is missing. In the repository ZIP that is `DramaFetch/extension`, and Windows' **Extract all** puts the ZIP's own top folder inside a folder of the same name, so there it is usually `<destination>\projects-main\projects-main\DramaFetch\extension`; for the artifact, the unzipped folder itself. Leave **Developer mode** on: turning it off disables DramaFetch (Chrome 153 marks it disabled, reason `unsupportedDeveloperExtension`), and turning it back on enables it again.
3. In `chrome://settings/downloads`, turn off **Ask where to save each file before downloading**: while it is on, Chrome opens a **Save As** dialog for every episode even though DramaFetch asks for none (`saveAs: false`), the run waits on each dialog, and cancelling one fails that episode.
4. Pin DramaFetch to the toolbar (the puzzle-piece button → the pin). Keep exactly one copy loaded: to update, replace the files in the SAME folder and press the reload arrow on DramaFetch's card in `chrome://extensions` — never **Load unpacked** a second folder (a new download unzipped as `… (1)` is one): two copies are two engines with separate pauses and cooldowns, so if you did, **Remove** the old card. Press the reload arrow only while the DramaFetch tab shows **idle** (top right) and no **Waiting** list: reloading closes the DramaFetch tab, so a run in progress stops (the next run resumes it — what is saved is skipped); then open DramaFetch again from the toolbar. Do not delete or move the folder: Chrome loads the extension from it. The version you have loaded shows on that card and next to the title of the DramaFetch tab and of the popup (`1.3.1`); every change ships with a new number and a line in [`CHANGELOG.md`](./CHANGELOG.md), so after an update check that the number moved.

**Use.**

- On a TikTok short-drama tab (any episode of the series), click the toolbar button → **Download this series**: the DramaFetch tab opens with that series listed — nothing is downloaded yet. Press **Download all available** there to take every free episode and every paid one your account has unlocked; or tick episodes and **Download selected** (a ticked locked episode is asked for anyway — TikTok decides, and it usually fails). The run's first pause is normal: 60–120 s, shown as the rest before the next series (`resting 95 s before the next series`) — listing the series was a job that contacted TikTok too, and two such jobs less than two minutes apart get the rest between them. Then the series is listed again in a background tab, and the downloads start.
- Or **Open DramaFetch** and paste links into the box, one per line — any episode of a series, or the bare series id; blank lines and `#` comments are skipped, a series listed twice is taken once, and a single-video link or anything else is refused naming its line before anything starts. **Download all** takes every series of the box, strictly one after the other, each listed right before its own downloads. **Load list** shows one series first: cover, number, length, quality, the `free` / `paid · unlocked` / `locked` badge with its hints, and **on disk** for what is already saved; a row's cover appears once the row is in view (until then an empty frame of the same size holds its place). A second **Load list** — or the popup's **Download this series** — for a series whose listing is already running or waiting queues nothing: `That series is already being listed; wait for the running job.`
- **Keep the DramaFetch tab open while it works.** It may sit in the background (a background tab's timers can make a pause longer, never shorter): while a job runs or waits, DramaFetch marks its own tab not discardable, so Chrome's Memory Saver does not discard it mid-run, and once nothing runs or waits — after a **Stop** or an error too — the tab is discardable again. Closing it stops the run (a TikTok tab it was listing a series in is closed too), and the next run resumes — what is saved is skipped. **Stop** ends a run at its next pause or between two episodes. While a job runs, do not browse TikTok heavily in other tabs of the same Chrome: those requests and the job's come from one session, and TikTok counts them together. There is one DramaFetch tab per browser; jobs queue and run one at a time (a second DramaFetch tab is read-only: it queues nothing, and the toolbar's **Download this series** lists the series in the first one).
- The tab shows the current series, the pause in force, a row per episode (in a long batch only the most recent ones, about 2 000: see **Hundreds of series** below; a short status — `downloaded`, `already on disk`, `locked, skipped`, `stopped`, `rate limited`, … with the status code, such as `skipped-exists`, as its tooltip — and the message), each series' report line and a batch table, in Chrome's language: in a Spanish Chrome every text is Spanish (`descanso de 95 s antes de la siguiente serie`, `completa: 3 de 4 (3 descargados) — 1 episodio de pago que tu cuenta desbloqueó (3), 1 bloqueado (4)`), otherwise English (`resting 95 s before the next series`, `complete: 3 of 4 (3 downloaded) — 1 paid episode your account unlocked (3), 1 locked (4)`). Where the command line would name a flag, the tab names its own checkbox: a series skipped as complete with locked episodes left ends `… unlocked more in the TikTok app since? Tick “Look again at series found complete in the last 24 hours” to look again`, a remembered DRM verdict reads `protected by DRM (found by an earlier run): DramaFetch does not decrypt it; tick “Also ask for locked episodes (TikTok decides; usually they fail)” to try again`. When a job ends, the episodes it did not download — every `failed` row, the ones a stop left `stopped` included — move out of that table into a block of their own, **Did not download (N)**, with their series, number, status and message and a button, **Retry the episodes that did not download (N)**, which queues them again: one job per series over exactly those episodes, the same as pasting the link and **Download selected** (the cooldown gate, the rest between jobs, a fresh list; an episode meanwhile on disk is skipped; a locked one is never asked for; an episode an earlier run found encrypted is skipped without a request). The button is one click per retry, never automatic, and a retry cannot bring back what TikTok does not serve: an episode that fails again with `TikTok serves this episode short: the whole file came (… bytes, as listed) but it plays 70 s of 106 s — …` is being served a file whose header plays only part of it — a window for an episode the account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side: if the TikTok app plays it whole, watch it there, then tick **Look again at series found complete in the last 24 hours**; if the app breaks at the same point too, no retry changes it (in a Spanish Chrome, `reproduce 69 s de 106 s` on the list and `TikTok sirve este episodio recortado: …` on the row). **Past runs** keeps the last 20 (a job you stopped: `stopped by you`, with no rows and so no retry; a series that failed or never started: its error), each with its own `Did not download: E05, E06` line and retry button. What is stored — each series' progress, the past runs — stays the Python tool's English; only the display follows Chrome's language.

**The report of what did not download.** When a job ends with at least one `failed` row and the setting **Save a report of the episodes that did not download** is on (the default), the tab saves one JSON file, `Downloads/DramaFetch/reports/<Series name> - did not download <YYYY-MM-DD HHMM>.json` (`<N> series - did not download …` for a batch of several; a name already taken gets ` (1)`), and says so under the block: `Report saved: <file>`. With the setting off — or to save it once more — the **Save the report** button next to the retry button saves the same report; it is disabled when the last job had no failed episode (**Load list** and a job in which nothing failed write none). For each episode the job did not download — the `failed` rows, the ones a stop left `stopped` and the `locked, skipped` ones — the file holds its status and message, a probable cause in English (`TikTok serves this episode short: the file is the whole episode by size but its header stops at 70 s of 106 s. Either a window for an episode this account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side — the list cannot tell: if the TikTok app plays it whole, watch it there and look again; if the app breaks at the same point too, no retry will change it`, `the file plays 70 s where the list announces 106 s, and its size is none the list announced — a shorter file than listed: a window or a preview clip for an episode this account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side; the list's marks for it: isFree=False; unlockPrice=30` for a shorter file of an unlisted size, `the transfer was cut short (…): a network or server problem, worth a retry`, `encrypted media (DRM): …`, `not attempted: the run stopped before this episode (…)`, …), every play URL of the episode DramaFetch considered (requested or not) as `scheme://host/path` with its HTTP status, content type, announced and received sizes and what it came to (`HTTP 404`, `truncated body (1234 of 5678 bytes)`, `verify failed: duration 70 s, expected 106 s`, `not requested: host not covered`, `saved`), every in-run re-discovery (`adopted`, `locked`, `not listed`, `refused`, `failed`) and the list entry TikTok served to your session, as it came (of a series' `locked, skipped` episodes, the first three by number: the others are described all the same, and the digest below lists them) — plus, for comparison, the first episode of the series that did download or was already on disk, and, per series, an `episodes_digest` of EVERY listed episode (number, access, hints, `video.duration`, `music.duration`, `music.original`, `video.size`, bitrate, `IsPreview`, `PreviewType` — no URLs), so a wrong access decision can be checked from the report alone. Those list entries carry time-limited signed video links, so review the file before sharing it; the requests it lists carry no URL query string (TikTok signs it) — the signed links are only inside the list entries, as TikTok served them — and it never holds a cookie, a token or a request header; it only records what the downloader did anyway — no extra request, no changed decision. It is the thing to attach when asking for help. The Python tool has no such report yet: `--dump-raw` (the list payloads) and the messages `download` prints are its diagnostics.

**Where the files go.** Into Chrome's download folder: `Downloads/DramaFetch/<Series name> [<series id>]/<Series name> - Episode 01.mp4`; the reports of what did not download into `Downloads/DramaFetch/reports/`. For Windows' path limits the folder name is at most 60 characters and the file name at most 110 before `.mp4`, the series name shortened first so `Episode NN` is never cut; characters Windows or Chrome refuse or change in a file name (`<>:"/\|?*~%`, control and invisible formatting characters, the lone half of an emoji that a cut title can leave) become spaces or are dropped, so that each name is exactly the one Chrome saves (Chrome itself would turn a `%` into `_`). Chrome never overwrites a file (a name already taken gets ` (1)`), and an episode Chrome's download history shows saved at exactly that path — the file still there — is skipped without a request. Before a job reads that history it has Chrome check that the files still exist — Chrome does so at most once every 10 s, so the job may first wait up to that long (a wait, never a request) — and a file deleted a moment ago is downloaded again. With the cover mode `both` or `file` the cover is also saved next to the video (`<name>.jpg`).

**Settings.** Quality — **Compatible** (the best H.264 rendition, plays everywhere; the default) or **Best** (the highest bitrate, any codec); cover — **embedded** (the default), **both**, **file** or **none**; file names — `{series} - Episode {number}` by default, with `{series}`, `{number}`, `{n}`, `{title}` and `{id}`; pace — a random pause or **human** (about one episode's length); and longer pauses only: between episodes and the rest every N episodes may be raised, never set below 4–9 s and 60–120 s every 10 (nor above one day, 86400 s, a pause); and **Save a report of the episodes that did not download** (on by default) — the JSON report under `Downloads/DramaFetch/reports/` at the end of every run with a failed episode; off, the **Save the report** button still saves it on demand. Per run, in the box: **Also ask for locked episodes** (asks TikTok with your session; nothing is unlocked) and **Look again at series found complete in the last 24 hours**.

**Pacing — the Python tool's floors, none lowerable.** One request at a time and one job at a time; 4–9 s between episodes (or the human pace: the previous episode's length plus 0–3 s, at most 300 s); a 60–120 s rest every 10 episodes that needed the network, and the same rest between two series that both contact TikTok; a 15–30 minute break every 100 network attempts (15 minutes without a request count as that break); 0.8–1.6 s before each episode-list page; at most 4 video URLs and 1 cover per episode (the hops of a redirect count as the URL they came from; a URL on a host the extension may not contact counts too, without being requested — as the Python tool counts it); a stop on HTTP 429, a captcha (met before the first list page or after it: a wall TikTok raises mid-list stops the job instead of a partial list being downloaded), a login wall or 3 failures in a row (across the series of a run) — an HTTP 403, 404 or 410 on a video URL, or a redirect to a host the extension may not contact, moves on to the episode's next URL, then to a fresh list, and only then fails the episode (`failed-unavailable`, counted toward the three), while a redirect loop, a sixth redirect or a request that gets no answer at all fails it at once (`failed`, `download failed: …`: no next URL, no fresh list) — after which every remaining series is `not started` with no request; and a 30-minute cooldown after a 429 or a captcha — a banner shows the minutes left, every TikTok action is disabled and no button lifts it — asked before every series and again right before every request; it is the extension's own, kept in Chrome's storage, and the Python tool's (`~/.dramafetch/cooldown.json`) is a separate one — neither sees the other, so after a refusal in one do not use the other on the same account until the time shown. A series found complete less than 24 hours ago, every file still on disk, costs no request at all. A fresh list costs one more listing of the series only when the run holds none for it yet or that list's entry for the episode is spent (every URL in it already tried, or expired) — never for an entry without URLs or an episode the fresh list lacks — and a listing that failed is not retried in the run: a long run lists a series again only as often as its play URLs run out. Between two jobs that contact TikTok — **Load list** is one too — comes the same 60–120 s rest whenever the previous one's last request, or its end (a stopped one too), was at most 120 s before, and every pause is slept to its end, however long. The run's pacing is kept in the extension's storage as it goes, so closing and reopening the tab resets neither the long break nor the rest between jobs.

**Hundreds of series.** DramaFetch sets no limit on how many series it keeps track of, nor on how many links one batch takes. Over a long project:

- **About 50 series per batch** is the comfortable size: at the default pauses a 60-episode series takes roughly 35 minutes, so 50 are about a day. Any stop — TikTok refusing a request, three failures in a row, **Stop**, closing the tab or Chrome, a Chrome update — ends the batch and leaves the series after it undone (after a refusal or three failures they read `not started`; after **Stop** the run keeps no rows, and a closed tab or Chrome keeps nothing): paste them again (a series already complete costs no request for 24 hours, and afterwards one listing and no download). Keep the whole list in a text file and paste the next block when a batch ends; the same tab can stay open for weeks.
- **A long batch stays light.** The progress table keeps the most recent episode rows (about 2 000) and says so under it, while every finished series keeps its report line, and every episode that did not download is listed at the end; a finished series' list entries are let go of as soon as the report has what it needs; the report keeps the list entry of at most three `locked, skipped` episodes per series; **Past runs** holds every run to its share (about 51 KB: a couple of 60-episode series kept whole), so that the last 20 runs stay together — a larger run is kept in a compact form, each series' line and its episodes that did not download (so its retry button works) without the other rows, the pauses and the reports, and a batch of more than about 200 series keeps its series with an episode to retry first, then as many others as fit, and says how many more it had (its retry button covers the series it kept; pasting the batch's links again fetches whatever is still missing).
- **Chrome's download list is part of DramaFetch's memory.** Until the project is over, do not clear it (**Clear all** in `chrome://downloads`, **Delete browsing data → Download history**, cleaner tools) and do not move or rename `Downloads/DramaFetch`: a series run afterwards would be downloaded again — after a cleared list next to the old files as `… (1).mp4`, after a moved or renamed folder into a new `Downloads/DramaFetch`. Keep the download drive connected while Chrome runs — if it was unplugged, restart Chrome before the next run.
- **Chrome itself grows with its download list.** With tens of thousands of downloads it starts more slowly, uses more memory and takes a few seconds at each series start to read the list (about 3 s at 56 000 downloads; twice as many with the cover mode `both` or `file`, which saves a second file per episode). A file deleted moments before a run may then still count as on disk once: run the series again, or tick **Look again at series found complete in the last 24 hours**.

**Two repeated requests per series.** The extension cannot read the responses of TikTok's own page, so it repeats, once each and from inside the page, the page's first episode-list request — its URL replayed unchanged, `cursor` and `count` included, so that TikTok's signature parameters still match the query they signed — and its series-detail request (the name and the announced number of episodes), and fetches the following list pages the same way: listing a series costs two small requests more than in the Python browser strategy, each after its 0.8–1.6 s pause. **Download all available** and **Download selected** never use the list on screen: another account — or none — may be logged in to TikTok by then, so they list the series again, after the rest between jobs (or the long break, when it is due), and that fresh list decides what is downloaded (an episode it now calls locked is skipped) — one listing more than in the web UI, whose list cache can tell sessions apart. The covers of **Load list** are fetched by the extension itself, and only for the rows in view (or within about one row, 64 px, of the window): scrolling brings the next ones, in the list's order, one at a time after the same short pause, never while a job runs or waits or the cooldown lasts, and they are kept for as long as the tab is open. A row scrolled past before its turn costs nothing, and a window too narrow to show the cover column fetches none.

**Built without access to TikTok.** Like the rest of DramaFetch, the extension was written and tested in a sandbox that cannot reach tiktok.com — against the fake TikTok server only (`tests/integration/test_extension.py`, `test_extension_network.py`, `test_extension_ui.py`, `test_extension_storage.py`). The first real run is the real test: start with **Load list** on one series and check the episode list before downloading a batch.

**How it works, and what it never does.** It lists a series by opening it in a new, inactive and muted tab of your Chrome, watching the URLs and status codes of the page's own list requests (never their content), and fetching the list from inside that page with the page's own `fetch` — your session's cookies apply exactly as they would for you, and the extension never reads them; the tab is closed when the list is read (also on any error, and when the DramaFetch tab is closed, reloaded or navigated away meanwhile). A verification wall (captcha) in that tab — while the first list is awaited, or later, when a list page brings no list or a scroll loads nothing — stops the job and starts the cooldown; the extension never solves it and never downloads from a partial list. It then downloads, from the DramaFetch tab, the play URLs that list gives your session, one at a time. Its own video, cover and thumbnail requests get `Referer: https://www.tiktok.com/` set and `Origin` removed — what the Python tool sends — by one session rule scoped to the extension's own requests, so the requests of TikTok's page are never touched. A redirect is followed only to a host the extension may contact, at most 5 hops, never from https down to http and never in a loop, each hop requested the same way (with the same two header changes), and it is learnt from the DramaFetch tab's own requests only — Chrome tells the extension nothing about your other tabs; a redirect to any other target counts as refused and the next URL is tried, while a loop, a sixth hop or a request that gets no answer fails the episode at once, as in the Python tool; the hops do not use up the episode's 4 URLs. Each body is checked in memory — complete, not encrypted, a valid MP4 with an audio track and the listed length — before anything is saved through Chrome's downloads. It never unlocks, buys or fakes entitlement; never sends anything back to TikTok, reuses a preview's or another account's URL, or re-signs or guesses a URL; never decrypts or strips DRM, and never keeps encrypted media; never patches, wraps or overrides anything in TikTok's page, uses `chrome.debugger`, reads cookies or changes a request of TikTok's page; and contacts no site but TikTok and its CDNs — no analytics, no remote code.

## Install

Python 3.11 or newer.

```bash
cd DramaFetch
python -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e ".[impersonate,dev]"   # impersonate: curl_cffi so yt-dlp presents a real browser's TLS fingerprint; dev: pytest, ruff, mypy
playwright install chromium           # the browser that discovers the episode list …
dramafetch doctor                     # … or skip it and use the Chrome/Edge you have: --browser-channel chrome|msedge
```

On Windows, in PowerShell (`py` is the Python launcher; `python` works too when it is on `PATH`):

```powershell
cd DramaFetch
py -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -e ".[impersonate,dev]"
playwright install chromium
dramafetch doctor
```

`dramafetch doctor` must end with every line ✓. Its `browser` line reads `Playwright Chromium at … (the full browser, launched headless; --browser-channel chrome uses your Chrome instead)` — Playwright's full Chromium, which DramaFetch launches headless by default ([Flags](#flags): `--browser-channel`) — and its `curl_cffi` line names the TLS fingerprint yt-dlp impersonates (`… as chrome-152:windows-10`); a `–` there instead means the default engine would send Python's own TLS fingerprint under a Chrome user agent — see [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting).

`ffmpeg` is optional: TikTok episodes are single progressive files, nothing needs merging.

## Cookies (you will need them)

1. Log in to tiktok.com in your browser and open an episode of the series to make sure you can watch it — for paid episodes, with the account that unlocked them ([Paid episodes](#paid-episodes)).
2. Export the site's cookies in Netscape `cookies.txt` format with a browser extension such as "Get cookies.txt LOCALLY" (Chrome/Edge) or "cookies.txt" (Firefox). Export only tiktok.com.
3. Save the file as `cookies.txt` where you run DramaFetch (any `*cookies*.txt` is gitignored in this repository) and pass `--cookies cookies.txt` to every command.

The file is a key to your TikTok account: keep it private, never share or commit it, and expect it to expire after some weeks — when discovery starts failing with login errors, export a fresh one. `dramafetch doctor --cookies cookies.txt` reports its age, how many `.tiktok.com` lines it has and whether a session cookie (`sessionid` / `sid_tt`) is present — names only, never values. It also flags malformed lines (not seven tab-separated fields, or a non-numeric expiry) by line number: yt-dlp would skip such a line with a warning that quotes it whole, cookie value included, so `download` and `video` refuse a file with malformed lines until it is fixed or re-exported. For the download step yt-dlp can alternatively read cookies straight from a browser profile with `--cookies-from-browser chrome` (`firefox`, `edge`, …); the discovery step still needs the file — and on Windows `chrome` and `edge` cannot read a current Chrome's or Edge's cookies at all, which are app-bound encrypted ([yt-dlp/yt-dlp#10927](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/10927)), so the exported `cookies.txt` is the working route there.

DramaFetch only reads the file. yt-dlp saves its cookie jar back into its cookies file every time it closes — once per episode — so it gets a private copy instead of your export: made in DramaFetch's state directory (`~/.dramafetch/yt-dlp-cookies-*.txt`, or under `DRAMAFETCH_STATE_DIR`; readable by you only) when the first episode is handed to yt-dlp, kept for the following episodes — the cookies TikTok sets along the way stay with it, like a browser session — and deleted when the run's downloads end. Your `cookies.txt` keeps its bytes and its modification time, so `doctor`'s age stays true and the web UI's list cache, keyed on the file's size and modification time, survives a yt-dlp job ([Web UI](#web-ui)). If the state directory cannot take the copy, a warning is logged and yt-dlp gets the export itself, which it then rewrites in place (its own header, the `#HttpOnly_` markers dropped, a new modification time, every cookie TikTok set for yt-dlp's client), as earlier versions did.

## Before your first real run

DramaFetch was written without access to TikTok, so your first run against it is the real test — the whole flow once, on Windows (PowerShell, the `.venv` activated), in this order:

1. `dramafetch doctor --cookies cookies.txt` — every line ✓: the `browser` line must say `(the full browser, launched headless; …)`, the `curl_cffi` line should name the fingerprint yt-dlp impersonates (`… as chrome-152:windows-10`), and the `cookies` line a session cookie.
2. `dramafetch info URL --cookies cookies.txt --strategy browser --dump-raw raw\` on ONE short free series. Compare `raw\item_list_1.json` with `tests/fixtures/item_list_page*.json` — the keys named in [`CLAUDE.md`](./CLAUDE.md#where-the-tiktok-facts-live-and-how-to-update-them) ("Where the TikTok facts live") — and read the `warnings` `info` prints: `scrolled the page instead` means the pagination request DramaFetch re-issued was refused — report it ([Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting)), with the `raw\` files, which never contain cookies.
3. `dramafetch video https://www.tiktok.com/@_/video/<item id> --cookies cookies.txt --out test` with one item id from `info --json`. If it ends `blocked (verification): …`, the default engine is challenged on this connection: use `--engine browser` for the series.
4. `dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --episodes 1-3`: each `done` line must end `+ cover embedded`, the files must be `.mp4`, play with sound in VLC and show the cover in a player that reads MP4 cover art (iTunes, the Apple TV app, Infuse, VLC's media library).
5. Only then whole series — one or two per sitting ([Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections)).
6. Never two DramaFetch at once — CLI, web UI or extension —, wait out any cooldown (`doctor` shows it), and remember that the extension's cooldown is separate from the Python tool's: a refusal met in one is not shown by the other.

## Paid episodes

DramaFetch downloads what TikTok serves to **your own session** — nothing else. A paid episode your account has unlocked — bought with coins, purchased, or part of a subscription — is downloaded like a free one, with the cookies of THAT account: export `cookies.txt` from the browser where the account that paid is logged in. An episode your account has not unlocked is listed and skipped.

What DramaFetch never does, whatever the flags: unlock an episode, buy one, fake or claim an entitlement, send anything back to TikTok to change what it serves, reuse a preview's URLs or another account's, re-sign or guess a URL, or decrypt DRM. Recording or decrypting a protected stream is circumvention of a technical protection measure, subscription or not; DramaFetch does not do it and never keeps encrypted media (see **DRM** below).

**How to tell.** `info` prints each episode's status from what TikTok sent to your session, and the web UI shows the same as a badge:

| `info` status | Web badge | What it means | What a download does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `free` | `free` | Nothing in the list says the episode is sold | Downloads it |
| `unlocked (paid)` | `paid · unlocked` | Sold, and your account may play it: the list carries an entitlement flag (`isUnlocked: true`, `hasPaid`, `payStatus: "paid"`, `lockStatus: "unlocked"`, …), or a play URL TikTok gave your session for a sold episode (an `http(s)` address — a rendition without one is none) with no flag that says otherwise | Downloads it with your session, like a free one; the report line names it (`1 paid episode your account unlocked (3)`) |
| `locked: <hints>` | `locked` and the hints | Your session cannot play it: a DRM flag (whatever else the list says; a key that names DRM in whole words — `isDrm`, `drmType`, `DRMType`, `is_encrypted`, `encryption` — with a value that is not false or `none`; never an HDR flag such as `hdrMode`, a negation such as `isDrmFree`, `no_drm` or `nonEncrypted`, or an identifier such as `encryptedUid`), or — when the list carries no entitlement flag — a short header (next row), a lock or paywall flag (`isLocked`, `needPay`, `lockStatus: "locked"`, …; `blockStatus` or `isBlocked` is no lock, and `IsPreview`, `PreviewType`, `isTrial` and `IsFreeIntro` are no clue at all: TikTok sets them on fully served episodes) or no play address at all, sold or not (for a sold episode, no play URL), or, on a sold episode, an entitlement flag that says no, such as `isUnlocked: false`. A free series with conditions (`isLimitedFree: true`; what the condition is, the field does not say) is `free` whatever price it names (the hint `isLimitedFree=True` next to that price) | Skips it (`skipped-locked`) without a request |
| `locked: plays 69 s of 106 s` | `locked` and that hint | The file TikTok would serve plays only part of the episode: the episode's own sound (`music.duration`, with `music.original: true`) is at most 85 % of `video.duration` and at least 1 s shorter — the file is the whole episode by size with a header that plays about 70 s: a window for an episode the account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side (the owner's 7 episodes of 2026-11-06 broke at the same point in the TikTok app and in TikTok's web player: no sound past it, a broken picture when seeking past it); the list cannot tell the two apart. Without `music.original` or either duration the rule never fires | Skips it without a request; if the TikTok app plays the episode whole, watch it there, then `--recheck`; if the app breaks at the same point too, nothing changes it until TikTok replaces the file |

`info --json` and the web UI's `/api/resolve` carry it per episode as `access` (`free`, `unlocked` or `locked`), `paid` (a price or an entitlement is mentioned) and `drm` (the list itself flags DRM), next to `locked` and `lock_hints`.

**A locked episode you want.** Unlock it in the TikTok app (or on tiktok.com) with the same account, export a fresh `cookies.txt` from that browser, and run the same command again: the manifest resumes — what is on disk is skipped without a request — and the episode now downloads. A series that a run found complete — with at least one episode on disk — is skipped for 24 hours without a request, and its report line says what is still locked: `complete, nothing to do: 3 episodes already on disk, 1 locked (4) (checked 2 h ago) — unlocked more in the TikTok app since? --recheck looks again`. So after unlocking episodes in the TikTok app, run with `--recheck` (CLI) or **Refresh list** (web UI).

**`--include-locked`** asks TikTok anyway, with your session — TikTok decides; nothing is unlocked. It is for an episode the heuristics below got wrong; a really locked one ends `failed-unavailable` (no formats, no playable stream), or `failed-unavailable` with `TikTok serves this episode short: the whole file came (… bytes, as listed) but it plays 70 s of 106 s — if the TikTok app plays it whole, watch it there, then --recheck looks again; if the app breaks at the same point, the episode is defective on TikTok's side and no retry changes it (kept as … (short).mp4)` when TikTok serves the whole file with a header that plays only part of it (the integrity check reads the duration; a short file of a size the list did not announce is `failed`, `download looks incomplete or wrong: …`, instead). DramaFetch never rewrites that header.

**DRM.** If TikTok ever protects paid episodes with DRM, DramaFetch says so instead of saving a file that cannot play: `failed-unavailable` with `protected by DRM (encrypted tracks): DramaFetch does not decrypt it`, and the file is deleted — that check always runs, `--no-verify` or not ([Integrity](#file-names-and-thumbnails)). Retrying cannot change the verdict, so the manifest remembers it: the next runs skip that episode as `skipped-locked` (`protected by DRM (found by an earlier run): …`) without a request, and `--include-locked` asks TikTok again. Only what a download found is remembered: a DRM flag in the episode list (`protected by DRM (<hints>): …`) is read again whenever the series is listed, so an episode TikTok stops flagging is simply tried then — at once with `--recheck`, otherwise once the 24-hour skip of a complete series has passed. And such an episode does not keep its series incomplete: like a locked one it cannot be downloaded, so a series whose only missing episodes are DRM-protected is complete — with at least one episode on disk it is skipped for 24 hours like any complete series, its line naming them as locked.

**The field names are inferred.** DramaFetch was written without access to TikTok: the fields that say "sold", "locked" or "unlocked" (`isFree`, `unlockPrice`, `isLocked`, `isUnlocked`, `unlockStatus`, …) are read by key-name heuristics (`catalog.access_of`), after the payload shapes seen in October 2026. A first real run is how to confirm them: if `info` shows a status you know to be wrong, run `dramafetch info URL --cookies cookies.txt --strategy browser --dump-raw raw/` and look for that episode's item id in `raw/item_list_<n>.json` (without `--strategy browser` the list may come from the page itself and land in `raw/hydration_<n>.json` instead; neither file ever contains cookies; review it before attaching it to a bug report).

## Usage

```bash
dramafetch doctor --cookies cookies.txt                   # environment check: Python, yt-dlp, curl_cffi, Playwright, browser, ffmpeg, cookies, mutagen, cooldown
dramafetch info URL --cookies cookies.txt                 # list the episodes: #, item id, duration, quality (e.g. 720p h264), disk (✓ already downloaded), title, status (free / unlocked (paid) / locked: <hints>), warnings, target folder
dramafetch info URL --cookies cookies.txt --json          # the same as JSON (feed it to scripts)
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt             # every free or unlocked episode into ./downloads/<Series name> [<series id>]/
dramafetch download URL1 URL2 URL3 --cookies cookies.txt  # several series, strictly one after the other, each resolved right before its downloads
dramafetch download --from-file series.txt --cookies cookies.txt   # the series listed in a file (below), after any URL on the command line
dramafetch download --from-file series.txt --cookies cookies.txt --recheck   # also re-check the series complete on disk (normally skipped for 24 h)
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --episodes 1-10,12,20-   # a selection: ranges, single numbers, open-ended "20-"
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --dry-run   # show what would be downloaded, touch nothing
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --pace human --engine browser   # a long series: pace like a viewer, about half the requests
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --name-format "{series} - Episode {n} - {title}"   # your own file names (placeholders below)
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --thumbnail both   # also keep each cover as a .jpeg next to its video (Plex, Jellyfin, Kodi)
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --thumbnail none   # the videos only, no cover at all
dramafetch download URL --cookies cookies.txt --quality best   # the highest bitrate whatever the codec (H.265 needs a capable player); default: the best H.264
dramafetch video https://www.tiktok.com/@user/video/<id> --cookies cookies.txt   # one ordinary video, no discovery: <id>.mp4, its cover embedded
dramafetch serve --cookies cookies.txt                    # the web UI on http://127.0.0.1:8765/
```

`URL` is the series URL (any episode of it) or just the bare series id. Short links (`vm.tiktok.com/…`) are not expanded: open them in the browser and paste the full address.

**Several series.** `download` takes any number of series — `download URL [URL ...]`, and/or `--from-file FILE`, read after the command line — and downloads them one after the other with the rest and the long break described in [Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections). A file lists one series URL or bare id per line, in UTF-8; blank lines, lines starting with `#` and anything after ` #` are ignored:

```text
# series.txt — what to watch offline this week
https://www.tiktok.com/shortdrama/episode/7699393770256782354/1   # The Billionaire's Secret Heiress
7699393770256782399                                              # Second Chance Bride
```

Every line and argument is parsed before the first request: one that is not a series URL or id — or is a single-video URL, or an id typed in other digits than 0-9, such as full-width ones — is a usage error (exit 2) naming the line (`series.txt:4: Not a TikTok short-drama or video URL: …`) or the argument (`argument 2: …`), and nothing is contacted. A series given twice is downloaded once (`note: duplicate: <id> given more than once; it is downloaded once`); `--episodes` selects episodes of one series, so it is a usage error with several. Each series prints a header (`[2/5] <name> [<id>]: 30 episode(s) -> <folder>`), its episode lines and its report line; after the last one a table lists every series with its status — `complete`, `incomplete`, `skipped (complete)`, `failed: <error>` or `not started` — and its counts (listed, done, on disk, locked, failed). One series prints as before, with the report line after its `Summary:` line.

**The report line** says how complete a series is after the run: `complete: 30 of 30 (28 downloaded, 2 already on disk)`, `complete, nothing to do: 30 episodes already on disk (checked 2 h ago)` (skipped without a request, see [Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections)) or `incomplete: 26 of 30 — 2 locked (5, 6), 1 failed (12); episode 17 missing from TikTok's list` (also what was not downloaded, a total TikTok exceeded, and why a run stopped: `; stopped: episode 2 failed-rate-limited`). The paid episodes your account unlocked are named before the locked ones — `complete: 3 of 4 (3 downloaded) — 1 paid episode your account unlocked (3), 1 locked (4)`, and an incomplete line lists them among its details the same way — and a series skipped as complete while its manifest recorded locked episodes says so: `complete, nothing to do: 3 episodes already on disk, 1 locked (4) (checked 2 h ago) — unlocked more in the TikTok app since? --recheck looks again` ([Paid episodes](#paid-episodes)). A series is complete when every free or unlocked episode TikTok lists is on disk, nothing failed, the list has no gaps (numbers 1 up to the highest listed or announced) and as many are listed as TikTok announces; locked episodes are named in the line but do not make a series incomplete — they cannot be downloaded (an episode an earlier run found protected by DRM is named as locked too, and it does not make a series incomplete either, although TikTok lists it as playable: it cannot be downloaded — with `--include-locked` it is attempted, and then a failure is a failure). With `--episodes` — or the web UI's **Download selected** and **Download all available**, which select episodes too — the line still describes the whole series: an episode outside the selection counts as already on disk when the manifest says so, and as locked when it is (a selection never marks the series complete for the 24-hour skip, though). `info` knows the same per episode: its `disk` column (`--json`: `on_disk` per episode and `on_disk_count`) shows ✓ when the manifest of the folder `download` would use, `<name> [<id>]`, says the episode is on disk and its file is still there — so a ✓ is an episode the download skips without a request.

### File names and thumbnails

Every episode is saved as `<Series name> - Episode <NN>.mp4` — `The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 01.mp4` — with its cover embedded in the file as its cover art (with `--thumbnail both` also, and with `file` only, as `<same name>.jpeg` next to it — `.jpg` / `.png` / `.webp` with `--engine browser`) — see **Thumbnails** below. `--name-format FORMAT` changes the name. The placeholders are replaced one by one and nothing else in braces is accepted (`{number:02d}` is an error: the padding is built in); the result is made safe for every OS — `/`, `\`, `:`, `|`, `?`, `*` and the like become spaces, so a format cannot create sub-folders, and a trailing dot or a Windows device name such as `CON` is defused — and cut to 150 characters:

| Placeholder | Expands to | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{series}` | The series name (at most 80 characters); `series-<id>` when TikTok gives none, `video` for `dramafetch video` | `The Billionaire's Secret Heiress` |
| `{number}` | The episode number, zero-padded to two digits | `01`, `12`, `100` |
| `{n}` | The episode number as is | `1`, `12`, `100` |
| `{title}` | The episode title — TikTok's description — at most 60 characters | `The Billionaire's Secret Heiress Episode 1` |
| `{id}` | The TikTok item (video) id | `7699400000000000001` |

A format must contain at least one of `{number}`, `{n}`, `{title}` or `{id}`: `{series}` alone would give every episode the same name, and every episode after the first would be skipped as already existing. `dramafetch video` has no series, so its default is `{title}`, which for a bare video URL is the item id — `7699400000000000001.mp4`. The manifest records the file each episode landed in, so a run resumes whatever format it uses; files from an earlier run under a _different_ format are found through the manifest only, not by name.

**Thumbnails.** By default the episode cover goes _into_ the MP4 (`--thumbnail embed`): it is written as the file's cover art — the `covr` metadata atom, the place iTunes, the Apple TV app, Infuse, VLC's media library and most music-style players read — so the video carries its poster wherever you copy it and no image file clutters the folder. Windows Explorer, Finder and phone galleries ignore that atom and show a frame of the video instead, and Plex, Jellyfin and Kodi prefer a poster file next to the video: that is what the other modes are for — `--thumbnail both` embeds the cover _and_ keeps it as `<same name>.jpeg` (`.jpg` / `.png` / `.webp` with `--engine browser`), `--thumbnail file` keeps only the file (nothing embedded), `--thumbnail none` asks for no cover at all. The embedding is done by [mutagen](https://mutagen.readthedocs.io/) — pure Python, a runtime dependency, the library yt-dlp itself uses for the job; no ffmpeg, no AtomicParsley — and it never touches a downloaded video in place: the cover is written into a copy named `<name>.mp4.embed.part` in the same folder, which then replaces the original atomically; if anything fails the copy is deleted and the video stays byte for byte as downloaded. Only JPEG and PNG can live in a `covr` atom; TikTok's covers are JPEG (`originCover`, `cover`) and only its `dynamicCover` is WEBP.

Each engine fetches the cover its own way. The default `yt-dlp` engine leaves that to yt-dlp's `writethumbnail` (no postprocessor, no ffmpeg): yt-dlp fetches the cover just before the video, names the file after the cover URL's own extension — `<same name>.jpeg` for TikTok's covers (a `dynamicCover` fallback lands as `.image` and is renamed after its bytes) — tries the next cover its extractor listed when one fails, and says nothing when none can be had; DramaFetch then embeds that file into the MP4 and, unless `both`, deletes it. With `--engine browser` the cover is one `GET` of the cover URL the episode list names (`video.originCover`, else `video.cover`, else `video.dynamicCover`), made only after the video is on disk and outside that engine's budget of 4 media requests per episode, never retried and never a second candidate; the body is embedded and/or written as `.jpg`, `.png` or `.webp` after the image type. Whatever happens to the cover, the episode stays `done`; its message — recorded in `dramafetch.json` and shown in the web UI row (the terminal line of a `done` episode prints the file path followed by this cover note, e.g. `E01  done  …/The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 01.mp4 + cover embedded`; `--dry-run` prints the message) — ends with one of these suffixes:

| Message suffix | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ` + cover embedded` | `embed`: the cover is in the MP4, no file next to it |
| ` + cover embedded + file` | `both`: in the MP4 and next to it |
| ` + cover file` | `file`: next to the video only, nothing embedded |
| `; cover: webp cannot be embedded; file kept` (yt-dlp) / `; saved as file` (browser) | A WEBP cover cannot live in a `covr` atom: it is kept as `<same name>.webp` instead, whatever the mode |
| `; cover: could not embed (<reason>); file kept` / `; saved as file` | mutagen is not installed (the reason says `pip install mutagen`), the file is not an MP4 mutagen can write to, or mutagen failed: the video is untouched and the cover is kept as a file, so nothing is lost |
| `; cover: too large, not kept` | Over 5 MiB: an error page or abuse, not a cover — neither saved nor embedded |
| `; cover: not available` | yt-dlp got no cover (it fetches it silently and reports nothing) |
| `; cover: not a JPEG, PNG or WEBP image; file kept` / `; cover: <OS error>; file kept` | yt-dlp wrote a `<same name>.jpeg` that is not an image (an error page) or the file could not be read: it is left as it is, nothing embedded |
| `; cover: HTTP 429 from <host>`, `; cover: HTTP 404 from <host>`, `; cover: <reason>` | `--engine browser`: the one cover request failed (a 429 there is logged as a warning; if TikTok is really limiting, the next video request stops the run as usual) |

With either engine an episode skipped because its video already exists does not go and fetch a missing cover (delete the video, or pass `--overwrite`, to get both), and `--dry-run` prints ` + cover` where a cover would be fetched. Without mutagen — `dramafetch doctor` then says `✗ mutagen: not importable — covers are saved as files only (pip install mutagen)` — `embed` and `both` fall back to the file and the message says so. The web UI's `thumbnail` flag means "a cover is available", embedded or as a file, and its **Cover** link serves whichever exists. A lone `<name>.jpeg` / `.jpg` never counts as "the episode exists": only `.mp4`, `.m4v`, `.mov`, `.mkv` and `.webm` do, and only under exactly the episode's name (`Ep 1.5.mp4` is never `Ep 1`'s file).

**Quality.** Audio and video come in one file: TikTok episodes are single muxed MP4s, so nothing is merged, nothing is re-encoded and ffmpeg is not involved — DramaFetch only _chooses_ among the renditions TikTok lists for the episode (`video.bitrateInfo`: a codec, a bitrate and a `GearName` such as `normal_720_0` or `adapt_lower_1080_1` each), and the source's top rendition is the ceiling. `--quality compatible` (the default) takes the highest-bitrate **H.264** rendition, which every player, TV and phone handles (yt-dlp: `best[vcodec^=h264]/best[vcodec=avc1]/best`; `--engine browser`: the H.264 play URLs by bitrate descending, then the other codecs, then `video.playAddr`). `--quality best` takes the **highest bitrate whatever the codec** (yt-dlp: `best`; the browser engine: every rendition by bitrate descending) — on TikTok that is often `bytevc1`, its H.265 variant, which is smaller and sharper but needs a player with H.265 support (VLC, mpv, recent TVs and phones; an older Windows box or a web browser may show a black picture). Play URLs whose path ends in `/media-video-hvc1/` are TikTok's video-only HEVC streams — no audio track: yt-dlp marks every such format `acodec: none` ([yt-dlp/yt-dlp#16622](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/16622), [#17372](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/17372)) and its `best` never takes one — and both engines and the Chrome extension leave them out in both quality modes; before, `best` could pick one, the file was quarantined as `no audio track`, and three in a row stopped the run. `info` prints a `quality` column with the rendition the default mode would take — `720p h264`, `1080p bytevc1`, `-` when the list names none — and `info --json` carries it as `quality_hint`.

**Integrity.** Every saved video is verified before anything else happens to it, so a half-downloaded or wrong file never passes for an episode (`--no-verify` turns this file check off; it is a debugging aid, nothing more). The check — done with [mutagen](https://mutagen.readthedocs.io/), the library that also embeds the covers; no decoding, no ffmpeg — asks four things of the file: it parses as an MP4; none of its atoms is cut short (a transfer that stopped early leaves an `mdat` shorter than its header says; mutagen alone would not notice, so the top-level atoms are walked); it has an audio track; and it lasts about as long as the episode list said (TikTok's `duration`, a whole number of seconds: a difference beyond 3 s or 5 % fails it — a 20-second file for a 2-minute episode is exactly what this catches; `dramafetch video` has no list, so its duration is not judged). When the file that plays too little is the whole episode by size — within 0.5 % of a size the list announced for it (`video.size`, a rendition's `DataSize`) — it is what TikTok serves for that episode, not a broken transfer: a window for an episode the account has not unlocked, or a defective encode on TikTok's side. The episode is `failed-unavailable` with `TikTok serves this episode short: the whole file came (<size> bytes, as listed) but it plays 70 s of 106 s — if the TikTok app plays it whole, watch it there, then --recheck looks again; if the app breaks at the same point, the episode is defective on TikTok's side and no retry changes it (kept as <name> (short).mp4)`, the file renamed `(short)` instead of `(incomplete)`, and a rerun will not change it: if the TikTok app plays the episode whole, watch it there and the next list reads it fresh; if the app breaks at the same point too, nothing changes it until TikTok replaces the file ([Paid episodes](#paid-episodes)). `--engine browser` additionally fails a transfer whose body is shorter than the announced `Content-Length` (of an uncompressed body: under `Content-Encoding: gzip` the header counts the compressed stream and the browser's own check applies), or that the server cut off, without trying another mirror (that is the network, not the URL; this transfer check is not part of `--no-verify`, and since nothing was saved there is no `(incomplete)` file for it). A file that fails is **not deleted but renamed** `<same name> (incomplete).mp4` (`(incomplete 2)`, … when that exists — nothing is ever overwritten) — with one exception: when the operating system refuses the rename (on Windows, a file an antivirus or indexer still holds), DramaFetch tries again twice within a second and then **deletes** the file instead, so that it never passes for the episode, and the message reads `(could not rename …: <error>; deleted, so that it does not pass for the episode)` (or, if not even that worked, `(could not rename or delete …)` and asks you to delete it by hand) —, the yt-dlp engine deletes the cover it had saved, no cover is fetched or embedded for it, and the episode is `failed` with `download looks incomplete or wrong: <reason> (kept as <name>)` — `not a valid MP4 (…)`, `truncated MP4 (mdat atom cut short: 1200000 of 13087000 bytes)`, `no audio track`, `duration 20 s, expected 120 s`. The quarantined file never counts as the episode (its name differs), the manifest records `failed` with the message, so the next run downloads the episode again; look at the `(incomplete)` file if you want to know what TikTok sent, delete it when you are done.

**Encrypted (DRM) media is never kept.** Before that check — and always, `--no-verify` or not — every video is checked for encrypted tracks: a walk of its MP4 box structure (box headers only, never the video data) looks for what an encrypted file (Common Encryption: Widevine, PlayReady, FairPlay) declares — an `encv` or `enca` sample entry in `moov/trak/mdia/minf/stbl/stsd`, or a `pssh` box at the top level or in `moov`. Such a file can never play, DramaFetch does not decrypt it, and it never keeps it for anything else to decrypt: the yt-dlp engine **deletes** it at once — not `(incomplete)`: no rerun makes it play — with the cover and any `.part` next to it, also when yt-dlp failed after writing it, and both engines do the same with such a file they find already under the episode's name (one whose deletion the operating system refused): it is never skipped as the episode (`skipped-exists`), so it is never counted as on disk, served or zipped by the web UI; the browser engine checks the body in memory, never writes it, and requests no other mirror, no fresh list and no cover (every mirror serves the same encrypted file). The episode is `failed-unavailable` with `protected by DRM (encrypted tracks): DramaFetch does not decrypt it` (if the operating system refuses the deletion, the message ends `(could not delete …)`: delete the file by hand). yt-dlp's own `This video is DRM protected` gets the same status and message (an error that also names a refusal — a login, HTTP 429 or HTTP 403 — is that refusal: the run stops, a 403 or 429 starts the cooldown, and nothing is remembered as DRM) — DramaFetch never sets yt-dlp's `allow_unplayable_formats`, so the formats yt-dlp knows to be encrypted are never even fetched. Like any failure it counts toward the three-in-a-row stop. Retrying cannot change the verdict, so the manifest remembers it: the next run skips the episode as `skipped-locked` (`protected by DRM (found by an earlier run): DramaFetch does not decrypt it; --include-locked tries again`) without a request; `--include-locked` asks TikTok again, with your session. Bytes that merely spell `encv` somewhere inside the video data are not boxes and mean nothing: an ordinary episode is never taken for an encrypted one.

### Output layout and resuming

```text
downloads/
└── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress [7699393770256782354]/
    ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 01.mp4   ← the video, its cover embedded as the MP4's cover art
    ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 02.mp4
    └── dramafetch.json        ← the manifest: status, path and message per episode
```

With `--thumbnail both` (or `file`) each episode also gets its cover as a file next to it:

```text
    ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 01.mp4
    ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 01.jpeg  ← the cover file (.jpg / .png / .webp with --engine browser)
    ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 02.mp4
    ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 02.jpeg
```

Several series (one `download` with several URLs, or `--from-file`) each get their own folder, side by side:

```text
downloads/
├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress [7699393770256782354]/
│   ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 01.mp4
│   └── dramafetch.json        ← also remembers when the series was last found complete
└── Second Chance Bride [7699393770256782399]/
    ├── Second Chance Bride - Episode 01.mp4
    └── dramafetch.json
```

The complete skip finds a series' folder by its id — the folder whose name ends in ` [<series id>]` and holds that series' `dramafetch.json` (the most recently written one, when a renamed series left two) — and only reads it. `info`'s `disk` column and the web UI's **on disk** badges read the folder the download will use, `<name> [<id>]`: after TikTok renames a series, its old folder's episodes are not marked, because the download goes to the new folder and fetches them again.

A video that failed the [integrity check](#file-names-and-thumbnails) stays next to the others under a name the tool never mistakes for the episode:

```text
    ├── The Billionaire's Secret Heiress - Episode 03 (incomplete).mp4   ← kept for inspection; the episode is `failed` and the next run downloads it again
```

File names follow `--name-format` ([above](#file-names-and-thumbnails); two digits, three from episode 100 up) and are sanitised for every OS. `dramafetch.json` is written after every episode, so an interrupted run — Ctrl+C, a 429, a dropped connection — resumes where it stopped, whatever name format it uses: run the same command again and the finished episodes are reported as `skipped-exists` without touching TikTok. What resumes is the series, not the file: an episode interrupted mid-download (`<name>.mp4.part`) is downloaded again from the start, never continued by byte offset — yt-dlp would append the tail of whatever rendition the new run selects to the head of the one the interrupted run started (a Range request; nothing compares the two files), and a longer such file passes the integrity check. `--overwrite` ignores the manifest. When a whole-series run finds the series complete, the manifest also records `complete_at` (UTC) and `complete_episodes`; within 24 hours the next run skips that series without resolving its list (see [Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections)).

**Windows paths.** Without long paths enabled, Windows refuses a path over 260 characters (`MAX_PATH`), and a long series title in a deep `--out` folder can push `<out>\<Series name> [<id>]\<Series name> - Episode 01.mp4.embed.part` past it: yt-dlp then cannot write the file (`failed`; three in a row stop the run), or the cover cannot be embedded and stays a sidecar file. Use a short output folder (`--out D:\dl`) or enable long paths (the `LongPathsEnabled` registry switch, or its Group Policy setting). The Chrome extension caps its own names (folder 60, file stem 110 characters), so it is not affected.

Per-episode statuses: `done` · `skipped-exists` · `skipped-locked` (including `protected by DRM (…)`: the episode list flags it, or an earlier run found it encrypted — no request) · `dry-run` · `failed-login-required` · `failed-unavailable` (locked, region-restricted, removed or private — or encrypted: `protected by DRM (encrypted tracks): DramaFetch does not decrypt it`, nothing kept) · `failed-rate-limited` (HTTP 429) · `failed-blocked` (HTTP 403, or yt-dlp's `Your IP address is blocked from accessing this post`: TikTok refused the request; or a JS challenge TikTok served instead of the video page, which DramaFetch never answers: `blocked (verification): TikTok answered the video page with a JS challenge, which DramaFetch does not solve; wait 30-60 minutes, then try --engine browser, which loads no video page`) · `failed` (including `download looks incomplete or wrong: …`, a video that failed the integrity check and was kept as `(incomplete)`). A login, 403 or 429 failure stops the run, and so do 3 failures in a row; the remaining episodes are marked `stopped: …` without contacting TikTok, and a 403 or 429 starts the 30-minute cooldown (see [Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections)).

### Exit codes

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Everything selected is done, skipped (exists / locked / a series complete on disk) or dry-run |
| 1 | Another DramaFetch error (the message says what) |
| 2 | Usage error (inconsistent pacing flags, an invalid `--name-format`, …), unsupported URL, or at least one episode failed — also a run stopped after 3 failures in a row, and a `download` that refused a `cookies.txt` with malformed lines before any request |
| 3 | TikTok wants a login: export fresh cookies and pass `--cookies` |
| 4 | Discovery failed: a captcha / "verify you are human" page (it also starts the 30-minute cooldown; afterwards try `--headful`), an HTTP error such as 403 for the series page, or no episode list arrived |
| 5 | Rate limited (HTTP 429) while listing the episodes, or — while downloading them — blocked (HTTP 403, `failed-blocked`; with the default engine also a JS challenge on a video page, `blocked (verification): … wait 30-60 minutes, then try --engine browser, which loads no video page`; with `--engine browser` also a captcha page met while refreshing the list) or rate limited (HTTP 429, `failed-rate-limited`): wait 30–60 minutes, then run the same command to resume (no other strategy is tried, nothing retries on its own; after a captcha, rerun with `--headful` and solve it once the 30-minute cooldown has ended). Also, with the same code, `info`, `download` or `video` refusing to contact TikTok at all because such a refusal happened less than 30 minutes ago — the **cooldown** (see [Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections)): the message says until when, and names the file to delete if you must |
| 130 | Interrupted with Ctrl+C — the manifest is saved; run again and the finished episodes are skipped, while the episode that was being downloaded starts over (its `.mp4.part` is never resumed by byte offset) |

A `download` of several series exits with the worst code among its series, in the order 5 > 3 > 4 > 2 > 0: a batch whose second series hit a 429 exits 5 even if the first was complete, and the series it did not start count with the code of the one that stopped it.

### Web UI

`dramafetch serve --cookies cookies.txt` starts a single-page UI on `http://127.0.0.1:8765/` — stdlib `http.server`, vanilla JS, no CDN, a phone-sized layout, your light/dark preference — that works like a paste-a-link downloader (SnapAny-style). One server per port: on Windows — where `SO_REUSEADDR` would let a second server bind a port that is already listening — the port is bound exclusively, so a second `dramafetch serve` on the same port fails at once with `error: cannot start the web UI on 127.0.0.1:8765: … address already in use` instead of the two silently sharing the page's connections (two workers, two downloads at once from one session). In three steps:

1. Paste the series URL (any episode of it, or the bare series id) → **Resolve**. The episode list appears with each episode's cover, length and `free` / `paid · unlocked` / `locked` badge (a paid episode your account has unlocked is ticked like a free one; locked ones are unticked and show their hints — see [Paid episodes](#paid-episodes)), plus an **on disk** badge on the episodes an earlier run already saved (what the series' manifest says, read without touching it; the count shows next to the title) — downloading them again costs no request, they are simply skipped. Resolving runs inside the request and takes 10–40 s while Chromium opens TikTok; the page says so while it waits. The list is then kept on the server for 15 minutes — resolving the same series twice within that time costs nothing (the status line says when the list was fetched), and the download step reuses it — and a small **Refresh list** link next to the title fetches it again. It is kept for the same `cookies.txt` only — the cache is keyed on the file's size and modification time, never its content: a file exported again (another account, or after unlocking an episode) gets a fresh list, while a yt-dlp job leaves the file untouched (yt-dlp works on a private copy, see [Cookies](#cookies-you-will-need-them)), so the cache no longer empties after every download job.
2. **Download all available** — one click: every free episode and every paid one your account has unlocked — or tick episodes and **Download selected** (a ticked locked episode is asked for anyway, like `--include-locked`); the list resolved a moment ago is reused, so this is no second visit to TikTok. Jobs run one at a time (more are queued) and are paced exactly like the CLI: `serve` takes the same pacing, engine, naming and cover flags as `download` (`--pace`, `--rest-every`, `--engine`, `--name-format`, `--thumbnail`, …). The files land on the computer running DramaFetch, under `--out`: `<Series name> [<series id>]/<Series name> - Episode NN.mp4`, its cover embedded in the video by default — `--thumbnail both` or `file` saves the cover as an image next to the video as well, or instead (see [File names and thumbnails](#file-names-and-thumbnails)).
3. Watch the progress table (polled every 2 s). Every row with a file — one this job downloaded (`done`) or one an earlier run already saved (`skipped-exists`) — gets a **Save video** link and, when a cover is available — embedded in the video or saved next to it — a **Cover** link: they copy the file (the embedded cover as a `.jpg` / `.png`) from the server to the device you are browsing from — a phone or a TV, for instance. Once the job is finished, **Save all (ZIP)** above the table copies every file of the job in one go — the whole series after **Download all available** or a batch: `<Series name>.zip` holding `<Series name>/<Series name> - Episode NN.mp4` (and the cover images saved next to the videos, if any), stored, never recompressed — the videos go in byte for byte, so picture and sound are untouched — and streamed 64 KiB at a time, so even a long series is never held in memory or copied to a temporary file on the server. The files themselves stay on the server; the page says where (delete one there, or start `serve` with `--overwrite`, to download it again).

**Several series at once.** The **Several series** box takes one link (or bare series id) per line — blank lines and lines starting with `#` are skipped, like `download --from-file` — up to 50 at a time; **Queue all** parses them and queues one job per series (a series listed twice is queued once) without contacting TikTok at all: each job resolves its own series right before its own downloads, through the same 15-minute list cache, never all the lists up front. The **Jobs** panel lists every job, the most recent first, refreshed every 2 s while one is queued or running: the series, its state (`queued`, `running` — with the pause it is in, e.g. *resting 95 s before contacting TikTok again* —, `done`, `error`, `cancelled`), how many of its episodes are processed (`12/30`), a `complete` / `incomplete` badge with the report line (`complete: 30 of 30 (28 downloaded, 2 already on disk)`, `incomplete: 27 of 30 — 2 locked (5, 6), 1 failed (12)`), **Save all (ZIP)** once it is finished and has files, and **Details**, which opens its progress table. Jobs — from the box or from **Download** — run one at a time and share the pacing of the whole server: between two jobs that both contact TikTok the server rests like it does every `--rest-every` episodes (`--rest-min`–`--rest-max`, 60–120 s by default) unless the previous one finished longer ago than that, and the long break every 100 episodes counts across jobs — when it falls between two batch jobs it is taken before the next list, in place of that rest. The first sign of trouble — HTTP 403 or 429, a captcha, a login wall, three failures in a row (counted across jobs, like the long break), a list that cannot be fetched — ends that job and cancels every job still waiting (`not started: batch stopped in <series> (<reason>)`), without a single further request; queue them again once the cause is gone (after a refusal, once the cooldown below has ended). A series whose manifest says it was complete less than 24 hours ago, every file still there, is skipped without any request (`complete, nothing to do: …`; `serve --recheck` resolves it anyway), and its files can still be saved, one by one or as a ZIP.

**After a refusal.** When TikTok has refused a request — HTTP 403, HTTP 429 or a captcha, met by the CLI or by a job of this server — DramaFetch does not contact TikTok again for 30 minutes (the cooldown described in [Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections)), and the web UI honours it like the CLI: **Download** answers `429` with the cooldown message — when TikTok refused, until when, and which file to delete to lift it early — and the page disables the download buttons and counts the minutes down (locally; nothing polls TikTok or the server for it). **Resolve** still shows a list fetched less than 15 minutes ago, because that involves no request, but a fresh list has to wait too. Jobs already queued behind the one that met the refusal are cancelled (`not started: batch stopped in <series> (<reason>)`) instead of knocking again; when the refusal was met elsewhere — by the CLI, or by a resolve of this server — the next job in line ends as `error` with the cooldown message before any request, and the ones behind it are cancelled; a job already downloading stops before its next episode (`stopped: cooldown until 14:32`), even in the middle of a long break, and cancels the ones behind it too.

**From a phone or TV on your home network.** The server binds to `127.0.0.1` by default, so only the computer it runs on can reach it. To use the page from another device on the same Wi-Fi, bind to every interface with an access token — `dramafetch serve --host 0.0.0.0 --token <long random string> --cookies cookies.txt` — then open `http://<pc-ip>:8765/` on that device and enter the token once: the page shows a small **Access token** form the first time an API call is refused, and a session cookie (`HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict`, 30 days; nothing in the page's own storage) remembers it. `DRAMAFETCH_TOKEN=…` in the environment works too (the flag wins). The token must be at least 8 characters of visible ASCII without spaces or quotes, and a `--host` other than loopback (`127.0.0.1`, `::1`, `localhost`) without a token is a usage error (exit 2) before anything is bound; the startup line says `token: set` or `token: none (loopback only)`, never the value. The token guards every `/api/*` route — resolving, downloading, job status, files, doctor — while the page itself is public; your TikTok cookies never leave the server either way. It is plain HTTP for your own LAN, not for the internet: nothing is encrypted, and the session cookie carries no `Secure` flag, since a browser would never send it back over plain HTTP — so bind beyond loopback only on a home network you trust. As before, the output directory is fixed by `--out` (the page cannot choose paths), every POST must be `Content-Type: application/json`, and only requests whose `Host` header names the bound host or localhost are answered — bound to every interface (`0.0.0.0` / `::`) the server cannot know its own addresses, so there it accepts any IP address but still no other host name: reach the page by IP, `http://<pc-ip>:8765/`, as above (a name such as `mypc.local` gets a 403).

JSON API, for scripts (with a token, send `Authorization: Bearer <token>` or the cookie; `GET /favicon.ico` alone is answered `204` with no body, token or not — the page has no icon, and the browser's automatic request is spared a 401): `POST /api/session {"token"}` → `204` + `Set-Cookie: dramafetch_session=…` (`401` when wrong; needs no token itself); `POST /api/resolve {"url", "refresh"?: true}` → the series and its episodes, each with its `cover_url` and its `access` (`free` / `unlocked` / `locked`), `paid` and `drm`, plus `from_cache` (the list was resolved less than 15 minutes ago and reused — the server keeps the 32 most recently resolved series, never a failed resolve) and `resolved_at` (when it was fetched, seconds since the epoch); `"refresh": true` fetches the list again; `POST /api/download {"url", "episodes": "1-5" | null, "include_locked": bool}` → `202 {"job_id"}` (it reuses a cached list too); `POST /api/batch {"urls": [...], "include_locked"?: bool}` → `202 {"batch_id", "jobs": [{"job_id", "series_id"}], "dropped": [{"index", "series_id", "reason"}]}` — 1 to 50 entries, each a series URL or bare id, parsed and never resolved by the request; a single-video URL or unsupported text is a `400` naming it (`"entry 3: …"`, `"index": 2`), duplicates are dropped, and a cooldown is the same `429` as `/api/download`; `GET /api/jobs` → `{"jobs": [...]}`, the most recent first, at most 100, each job's snapshot without its `results` plus `counts` (rows per status), `processed` and `files` (rows with a file); `GET /api/jobs/<id>` → `{state: queued|running|done|error|cancelled, batch_id, series_name, total, report, pause, results: [{number, title, status, message, path, file_name, thumbnail}], …}` (`batch_id` is null for a `/api/download` job; a batch job knows its `series_name` and `total` once its turn comes; `report` is the series report — `listed`, `done`, `on_disk`, `locked`, `failed`, `gaps`, `complete`, `skipped_complete`, `stopped`, `unlocked` and `unlocked_numbers` (the paid episodes your account unlocked among those obtained), … and its `line` — null until the job ends; `pause` is `{kind: job-rest|pace|rest|long-rest, seconds, since}` while the job waits; a cancelled job never started, its `error` says why; `thumbnail` means a cover is available for that row — embedded in the video or saved as a file next to it — and is settled once, when the row is recorded, not at every poll); the series that `/api/resolve` answers also carry `on_disk` per episode and `on_disk_count`; `GET /api/files/<job id>/<number>` → the video of that row as an `attachment` (`Content-Type` from the suffix, `Content-Length`, `Content-Disposition` with `filename` and `filename*`, streamed in 64 KiB chunks) and `GET /api/files/<job id>/<number>/cover` → its cover: the `<same name>.jpg|.jpeg|.png|.webp` file next to the video when there is one, otherwise the cover embedded in the video, sent from memory as `image/jpeg` or `image/png` with its `Content-Length` and a `Content-Disposition` naming `<video name>.jpg|.png` — both only for `done` and `skipped-exists` rows whose path resolves inside `--out` (never a `.part`; the video is opened only after that check); `GET /api/files/<job id>/all` → every such file of a finished job as one `application/zip` (`Content-Disposition` naming `<series name>.zip`, entries `<series name>/<file>`, `ZIP_STORED` with ZIP64, written as a stream: no `Content-Length`, `Connection: close`; each entry passes the same check, so a path outside `--out` is never included), `409 {"error": "the job is still running"}` while it is queued or running; anything else is `404 {"error": "no such file"}`; `GET /api/doctor`. Bodies are capped at 64 KiB; errors are `{"error", "kind", "hint"?}` with a 4xx/5xx status (401 with `kind: "auth"` for a missing token, with `LoginRequiredError` for a TikTok login; 429 with `kind: "cooldown"` and a `Retry-After` header in seconds while TikTok is not to be contacted after a refusal — from `/api/download` always and from `/api/resolve` unless the list is cached — and 429 with `RateLimitedError` for a rate limit met while resolving; 502 discovery failed; 503 captcha).

### Why not a public site (SnapAny-style)?

A hosted "paste a link, get the file" site would have to do four things this tool deliberately does not. It would hold visitors' TikTok cookies — the key to their accounts — on a third-party server, because TikTok serves the episode list only to a logged-in session. It would call TikTok from a datacenter address, which is exactly what TikTok's protection targets, and one busy address would be rate-limited or banned for every visitor at once. It would relay every episode through that server — gigabytes per series, for every visitor. And it would carry the Terms-of-Service and takedown exposure of redistributing the studios' videos. DramaFetch instead runs where the session and a home IP already are, your own computer, and the web UI simply lets the other screens in your house use it.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause → what to do |
| --- | --- |
| `No video formats found` (status `failed-unavailable`) | Not logged in, the episode is locked, or it is not available in your region → check that `info` says `free` or `unlocked`, refresh `cookies.txt` (for a paid episode, from the browser of the account that unlocked it); `--include-locked` only confirms that a locked episode fails |

## Limitations

- No paywall, DRM or entitlement bypass, ever: episodes your account has not unlocked are listed and skipped (`--include-locked` asks TikTok anyway, with your session — TikTok decides), and encrypted media is never decrypted or kept.
- Short links (`vm.tiktok.com`, `vt.tiktok.com`, `tiktok.com/t/…`) are not expanded.
- TikTok changes its web app without notice, and the field names that say whether an episode is free, unlocked or locked for your session are heuristics (`catalog.access_of`; [Paid episodes](#paid-episodes)); a DRM flag counts only when a key names DRM in whole words (`isDrm`, `drmType`, `encrypted`), never an HDR flag, a negation such as `isDrmFree` or an encrypted user id — the check of every downloaded file is what really keeps encrypted media out. When something breaks, `info --dump-raw raw/` saves the raw payloads to adjust the parsers against.
- Built without network access to TikTok: the fixtures mirror payloads observed in October and November 2026 (the free series with conditions on 2026-11-06) and the integration test drives a real Chromium against a fake TikTok. The first real run is the real test.
- No parallel downloads, by design; the pacing defaults are a floor to raise for long series — the flags do accept lower values (the tests against the fake server need them), but nothing below the defaults is safe against TikTok (see [Pacing](#pacing-and-tiktoks-protections)).

## Development

```bash
cd DramaFetch && . .venv/bin/activate
ruff check . && ruff format --check . && mypy && pytest   # the definition of done
pytest -m integration                                      # a real Chromium against tests/integration/fake_tiktok.py
PYTHONPATH=src python -m dramafetch doctor                 # run the CLI from the checkout without installing
cd extension && node --test && npx -y -p typescript@5.9.3 tsc -p jsconfig.json   # the Chrome extension (Node 22)
```

`pytest -m integration` runs only when `DRAMAFETCH_CHROMIUM` points at a Chromium binary or `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium` exists (the cloud sandbox); after `playwright install chromium`, export it the way CI does: `DRAMAFETCH_CHROMIUM="$(python -c 'from playwright.sync_api import sync_playwright; p = sync_playwright().start(); print(p.chromium.executable_path); p.stop()')"`. Without it the browser tests are skipped, not passed; likewise the extension's parity and media tests need `node`, and the media tests' frame-checksum proofs a full ffmpeg (H.264 + AAC). With `DRAMAFETCH_REQUIRE_TOOLS=1` a test that would skip for a missing node, ffmpeg or Chromium fails instead. CI runs all of this in the `dramafetch` job of [`../.github/workflows/ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/ci.yml), with a full ffmpeg installed and `DRAMAFETCH_REQUIRE_TOOLS=1` set. Rules for agents, the module map and the maintenance agents: [`CLAUDE.md`](./CLAUDE.md).

`DRAMAFETCH_STATE_DIR` relocates DramaFetch's state directory (default `~/.dramafetch`) — `cooldown.json`, the file behind the 30-minute cooldown, and, while a yt-dlp run lasts, yt-dlp's private copy of the cookies file (`yt-dlp-cookies-*.txt`, deleted when the run's downloads end); every test runs with it pointed at a temporary directory (the autouse fixture in `tests/conftest.py`), so no test, unit or integration, ever touches your real cooldown. The fake TikTok server has two switches for the integrity check, `truncate_media` (the CDN announces the whole file and stops at 60 %) and `short_media` (the list keeps the fixtures' 92-second durations while the CDN serves the 3-second fixture), one for the discovery, `challenge_after_first_page` (every `item_list` page after the first — DramaFetch's own pagination fetch and the page's scroll handler's alike — is answered with the captcha page, after which the page shows the wall), and one for DRM, `drm_media` (a set of (series id, episode number) whose media the CDN serves encrypted: `tests/encrypted_mp4.py` renames the fixture's sample entries `encv`/`enca` and adds a `pssh` box — a structural edit, no binary fixture, nothing actually encrypted); the lists it serves otherwise say `duration: 3`, what its media really lasts.

Hidden flags on `info`, `download` and `serve`, for tests and diagnostics (not in `--help`): `--base-url URL` points discovery (and, with `--engine browser`, the download engine's refresh of expired play URLs) at another host such as the fake TikTok server, `--page-count N` (default 30) and `--max-pages N` (default 60) bound the `item_list` pagination, `--page-delay S` (default 0.8) is the minimum pause between pages (the pause actually taken is 1–2 times that, randomised). To drive the CLI against the fake server from a checkout: start `tests/integration/fake_tiktok.py`'s `FakeTikTok` in a Python shell, then `PYTHONPATH=src python -m dramafetch info 7699393770256782354 --strategy browser --base-url http://127.0.0.1:<port> --browser-path /opt/pw-browsers/chromium --page-count 10`.

Layout: `src/dramafetch/` — `urls` (parse what is pasted), `discovery/` (browser, static, cookies), `catalog` (pure parsing of TikTok payloads, and `access_of`: free / unlocked / locked), `manifest` (resumable runs), `downloader` (yt-dlp), `downloader_browser` (the browser engine), `media` (the integrity check of a saved video, the `(incomplete)` quarantine, and the DRM check: encrypted tracks found in the box structure), `cooldown` (the 30-minute "do not contact TikTok" file), `thumbnails` (the cover: sniffing, the sidecar, embedding into the MP4 through mutagen, reading it back), `engine` (the seam shared by both front ends: pacing, early stops, the cooldown recording, `make_downloader`), `cli`, `web/` (stdlib server, job queue, `static/index.html`). The Chrome extension lives in `extension/` (`src/lib` the pure ports and `display`, which shows their texts in English or Spanish; `src/app` the extension pages and the engine; module map in [`CLAUDE.md`](./CLAUDE.md#extension-extension)). Tests live in `tests/`, payload fixtures in `tests/fixtures/`, the fake TikTok server in `tests/integration/`. `tools/upstream_watch.py` (standard library only, tested in `tests/test_upstream_watch.py`) is the upstream watch the maintenance agents run through `../.claude/scripts/dramafetch-watch.sh`: what changed in yt-dlp, Playwright, mutagen, curl_cffi and Chrome, read from primary sources only, never from TikTok.
